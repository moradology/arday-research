# *Attempting to break the chain*: methodology manifestation and audit

## Full-text manifestation

The University of Kent Academic Repository record for Arday, Dina Zoe Belluigi, and Dave Thomas’s *Attempting to break the chain* identifies the item as a “Publisher pdf,” gives DOI `10.1080/00131857.2020.1773257`, and links a public 18-page PDF. The local copy includes the article’s methodology on PDF p. 9 and the article’s original reference list. The repository page says the file is the publisher PDF; this archive preserves that description without independently proving byte identity with Taylor & Francis’s CDN.

The local shell required TLS certificate verification to be disabled because the Kent host presented a self-signed certificate. The repository URL, response headers, record HTML, and file hash are preserved so that this retrieval condition is visible and reproducible. A browser-backed fetch returned a 502 for the direct document route during this pass.

## Bounded methodology comparison

The 2024 publisher correction [SRC-297](2024-05-16-educational-philosophy-correction.md) says that paragraphs 1 and 3–5 of the article’s methodology on printed pp. 303–304 were amended to clarify the relationship to Arday’s 2018 study and Memon et al. (2016). The preserved article text contains the sentence: “The initial part of the study involved each participant being given an anonymous self-administered questionnaire to complete which was deposited into a ballot box.”

The 2018 *Understanding Mental Health* article contains the same distinctive sentence and procedure description in its Materials and Methods section (PDF p. 10): “The initial part of the study involved each participant being given an anonymous self-administered questionnaire to complete, which was deposited into a ballot box.” Both passages then move into questionnaire content, focus-group material, participant composition, and audio-recording/transcription details, but the studies differ in sample size, participant population, research topic, and surrounding wording.

This is a direct sentence-level relationship in two public primary texts, and the correction independently records that the later article’s methodology was amended to clarify its relationship to the 2018 study and Memon. It is stronger than a generic shared method label, but it still does not determine whether the wording reflects legitimate authorial reuse, an editorial correction, source attribution failure, or misconduct. The correction’s addition of references is itself an important version-history fact.
