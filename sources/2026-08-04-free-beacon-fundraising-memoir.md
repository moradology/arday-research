# Washington Free Beacon: fundraising and memoir source-version report

Benjamin Ryan’s *Washington Free Beacon* article, published 4 August 2026, presents a 16-year comparison of public fundraising claims, biographical copy, and memoir-production material. It reports that the claims escalated from more than £100,000 in 2010, to £4.5m for 80-plus charities in a 2021 TEDx biography, to just over £5m in a 2021 talk, and to £5.5m in a 2024 speech. It also reports later clarification to the *Times* that Arday was part of a roughly 100-person syndicate and did not raise all the money alone.

The article links two Wayback snapshots of the Simon & Schuster Speakers Bureau biography. Those snapshots were independently retrieved and preserved here:

- **19 May 2025 snapshot:** says Arday had raised “over £5.5 million” for 80 national charities, lists 30 marathons in 35 days, 300 miles in 3 days, and 600 miles in 6 days, and explicitly says he undertook relief work in South America and West Africa with WaterAid installing water points.
- **24 July 2025 snapshot:** says he had been part of fundraising teams that raised “millions of pounds,” removes the specific totals and running feats, and retains more general wording about relief work.

The page-level change is directly verifiable from the two archived HTML captures. The article’s interpretation that the change was a quiet institutional or publisher response remains a reported journalistic characterization unless the edit history or responsible editor is independently documented.

The article also reports that it reviewed a university-job CV claiming just over £1.5m, and that a 2023 podcast included a claim of having been offered and declined an MBE; it says the *London Gazette* honours lists contained no such award. The CV, underlying podcast recording, correspondence, and any offer record were not recovered in this capture. These remain leads, not settled negative findings.

Finally, the article reports that the U.S. proof and U.K. final edition of *Great and Unfortunate Things* differed in their treatment of specific fundraising totals and targets, and quotes a Simon & Schuster spokesperson defending the book’s editorial/legal research process. The two book proofs were not publicly downloadable from the article and are not claimed as locally preserved documents.

## Evidence boundary

This record establishes the article’s publication and the exact wording differences in the two archived speaker-bio snapshots. It does not independently establish the aggregate fundraising total, the truth or falsity of every underlying event, the contents of the unpublished CV, the existence or nonexistence of an MBE offer, or the contents of the book proofs. The archived bio change is evidence of changing public copy; it is not by itself proof of why the copy changed or who authorized it.
