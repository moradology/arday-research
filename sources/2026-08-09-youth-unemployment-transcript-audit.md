# Youth Unemployment Committee: testimony audit

## Hearing identity and speaker boundary

The official corrected transcript identifies a virtual House of Lords Youth Unemployment Committee session on Tuesday 29 June 2021, evidence session 19, Questions 195–207. It names Dr Jason Arday as a Trustee of the Runnymede Trust and records Dr Gurleen Popli as the other witness. The transcript is corrected public evidence and says the session was publicly webcast.

At Q195 Arday introduces himself as associate professor of sociology and deputy executive dean for people and culture at Durham University. This is a dated hearing self-introduction/source-version. It is not current-status evidence and does not override later institutional or legal records.

## Arday’s testimony

- **Q195–Q196:** Arday describes youth labour-market precarity, racialised unemployment and hiring inequality, then advocates targeted partnership programmes, positive action, mentorship, guidance, and coaching for Black and minority ethnic people.
- **Q198:** He proposes a national databank of successful Black and minority ethnic people who could act as role models, consultants, or advisers. He links the need for such infrastructure to missing social capital and navigational knowledge, and says informal mentoring is difficult to measure consistently.
- **Q200–Q201:** He rejects framing racial inequality and white working-class disadvantage as competing problems, argues that education should support social mobility, and describes himself as an ex-PE teacher. These are public positions and first-person role testimony.
- **Q203–Q204:** He argues that placement providers need racial and intersectional literacy and that the “hidden curriculum”—including informal life-skills conversations around school—can be important to students. He presents this as experience-informed educational analysis, not as a disclosed study result.
- **Q205:** He says he has two African parents and describes a perceived parental preference for academic routes as a response to inequality, while acknowledging that academic and vocational routes are both important. This is first-person family testimony, not an independent demographic or family record.
- **Q206–Q207:** He recommends continual training for labour-market gatekeepers so they become intersectionally and racially literate, and agrees with embedding data skills in education.

## Evidence boundary

The transcript is primary evidence for what the committee recorded Arday saying and for the questions asked in the public hearing. It is not a study authored by Arday, does not expose a new dataset or method, and does not establish the empirical truth of every statistic or generalisation mentioned by either witness. Claims about unemployment, hiring discrimination, social mobility, or intervention effects require their cited source records or independent verification.

The committee’s later report is a related manifestation of the same hearing, not a second testimony source.
