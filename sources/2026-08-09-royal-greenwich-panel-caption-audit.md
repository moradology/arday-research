# Royal Greenwich Black History Month panel — Arday opening audit

The moderator introduces Dr Arday as the first speaker at approximately 00:48 and asks for a five-minute provocation. Arday speaks from roughly **00:55–07:22**, then hands over to Martha Holder. This audit covers only that opening contribution. The official English captions are preserved and readable, but remain automatic-caption evidence rather than a human-verified transcript.

## Subject-centred passages

- **00:55–02:49 — C-1119:** Arday discusses Britishness, belonging, and “historical amnesia,” arguing that accounts of British history often omit Black and ethnic-minority contributions to empire, infrastructure, the economy, and British life. He connects this omission to classroom belonging and to curricula that do not reflect multicultural and multi-ethnic society. This is a dated public argument, not an independent historiographical finding.
- **02:49–05:58 — C-1120:** He argues that embedding Black history as part of British history—not as a separate history removed from a white-dominant Eurocentric narrative—benefits all learners. He frames education as preparation for participation in society and links curriculum representation to attainment and educational outcomes. Captions also preserve his cited quantitative claim that the higher-education attainment gap was 23 percent and his statement that institutions had reduced it to varying degrees; the figures remain source-version claims until the underlying dataset or cited reports are checked.
- **05:58–07:22 — C-1121:** Arday discusses unequal access and progression, the marginalisation of non-dominant knowledge, and a possible education-to-prison “pipeline.” He cites high exclusion rates for young Black boys aged 13–16 and references 2011 Census figures about the Black and ethnic-minority population and prison representation. These are attributed presentation claims, not independently validated statistics or a causal finding.
- **00:55–07:22 — C-1122:** Across the opening, he presents belonging as something that must be established pedagogically in classrooms and treats curriculum, attainment, and institutional structure as connected rather than separate issues. This is a concise record of his public intellectual framing in the event, not a new personal biography claim.
