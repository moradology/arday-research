# Recovered report

The May 2021 *Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Race Equality Review* names Dr Jason Arday and Dr Lizzy Craig-Atkins as authors. It says BABAO commissioned the review in July 2020 and that Arday undertook it with Craig-Atkins and Rebecca Redfern supporting the review team. The report’s 25 pages include an executive summary, methodology, findings, conclusions, and a 27-recommendation implementation plan.

The report describes a mixed-method organisational review: 2019 equality-monitoring data from 116 responses, open-ended questionnaire material, focus groups involving 20 members, and stakeholder engagement. It records a substantial limitation: the participant pool was predominantly white and the review could not directly capture many lived experiences of members of colour. Its findings and recommendations are therefore preserved as a commissioned organisational analysis, not treated as a representative population survey or independent audit of BABAO.

The BABAO commissioning page states that the review cost £10,000 and that Arday agreed to support two members from Black and minority ethnic backgrounds to attend the 2021 BABAO conference. This is a contemporaneous institutional statement about the commission and proposed support, not independent evidence that the funding or attendance occurred.

## Follow-up topology

BABAO’s 2021 annual review says the report was completed after consultations in December 2020 and January 2021, published as a 25-page report, and adopted as a work plan with 27 recommendations. It identifies five pressing actions being worked on, including feedback processes, an inclusive strategy, racially literate recruitment, decolonising charitable activity, and culturally responsive support for racism victims.

BABAO’s 2022 annual review records work on six key performance indicators from the race review. It reports KPI 2 as paused, KPI 5 ongoing, KPI 6 complete after publication of an EDI vision statement, KPI 7 ongoing, and KPIs 14–15 ongoing with a hoped-for completion in the first half of 2023. This is useful implementation evidence but remains BABAO’s own annual-report account; it does not establish that every action was completed or that Arday remained involved.
