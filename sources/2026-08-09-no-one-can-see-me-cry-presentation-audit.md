# Page-level audit

This is a 12-slide Centre for Global Higher Education deck dated Thursday 2 July 2020. It is a distinct public research-talk artifact, not a second journal publication. The final slide identifies the associated paper as under review; the later *Higher Education* article remains canonical under [SRC-049](2021-no-one-can-see-me-cry.md).

## What the deck shows

- **Identity and setting — slide 1:** the title covers mental-health issues for Black, Asian and ethnic-minority academics, professional staff, and students in higher education. The title slide identifies Jason Arday as “Dr Jason Arday FRSA,” with Durham Sociology, CGHE, and Oxford affiliations dated to the talk.
- **Research framing — slides 4–6:** the deck presents the discussion as part of a qualitative mental-health study concerning racial discrimination in the academy and its impact on mental health. It frames racism as cumulative and discusses institutional barriers to contextually appropriate support, citing both Arday’s work and external literature.
- **Reported participant material — slides 2–3 and 8–10:** five anonymized quotations are presented, attributed only by broad identity/role and an age-like number. The deck does not identify participants, institutions, transcripts, or recruitment routes. The quotations are presentation content attributed by the deck, not independent evidence of the quoted events.
- **Recommendations — slide 11:** the deck calls for targeted psychological interventions, context-sensitive approaches including CBT, mindfulness-based CBT, and EMDR, and a more diverse healthcare-professional pool that does not decentre racism as a problem. These are the speaker’s recommendations, not an intervention evaluation.
- **Publication status — slide 12:** the deck lists “No One Can See Me Cry” as a forthcoming paper in *Higher Education* and says it was under review. This supports a dated presentation-to-publication relationship, not a claim that the slides and later article are textually identical.

## Method and version boundary

The deck names a qualitative study but does not disclose a sample size, recruitment method, interview count, focus-group design, ethics approval, coding procedure, or dataset. Those details appear in the later journal article’s reported methods and must not be back-projected into this 2020 presentation as if they were exposed here. Conversely, the journal article should not be treated as independent corroboration of every slide quotation: both artifacts may derive from the same underlying study.

The title slide’s Durham/CGHE/Oxford wording is a dated source-version biography, not current employment evidence. Spelling, typography, and wording are preserved in the local text derivative; the PDF controls for exact visual quotation.
