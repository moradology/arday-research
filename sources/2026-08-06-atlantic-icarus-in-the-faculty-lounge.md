# The Atlantic: “Icarus in the Faculty Lounge”

Daniel Engber's 6 August 2026 Atlantic feature examines Jason Arday's resignation, the forthcoming memoir *Great and Unfortunate Things*, and a 2024 book proposal obtained by the author. It is a major post-publication source-version for the biography, but it is not a clinical record, university finding, or independent verification of the memoir's personal narratives.

## Biographical material reported from the memoir

The article says the memoir opens with Arday's account of a 2006 Brazil trip, where he installed water pumps and was told by a local aid worker that he would suffer and do great things. It reports the memoir's descriptions of early autism/developmental-delay assessments, delayed speech and literacy, childhood self-regulation behaviours, support assistants in secondary school, a girlfriend helping with his university application, a friend sharing university notes, and his reading difficulties during postgraduate study.

It also reports memoir accounts of severe adolescent assault, later health problems including brain tumours and a post-operative ministroke, working overnight supermarket and early-morning cleaning shifts while teaching, and writing the PhD during long London–Liverpool train journeys. The article reports a memoir account of autistic burnout and a suicide attempt during the pandemic, but this is retained as sensitive, attributed memoir material rather than a verified clinical or incident record.

## Proposal/memoir discrepancies

The feature says the proposal and published memoir differ on several major particulars: the proposal reportedly describes a 2005 prophecy from a different figure, a teenage car collision followed by a three-month coma and locked-in syndrome, and testicular cancer; the published memoir instead describes two brain tumours and omits the coma narrative. The article also reports differing ages for the alleged attackers and different timing for the suicide attempt. These are discrepancies reported by The Atlantic between two narrative documents; the proposal itself is not locally claimed because no public binary or lawful full text was exposed.

The article further reports that editors, the co-author, and the agent did not answer its questions, while Simon & Schuster said publication would proceed. That is a record of the journalist's attempted contact and the publisher's limited response, not proof of the underlying allegations or of the truth/falsity of any memoir passage.

## Evidence boundary

This source is valuable for locating precise contradiction targets and for preserving a dated account of the memoir's public contents. The personal details above should be cited as “The Atlantic reports that the memoir says…” or “the article reports a discrepancy between the proposal and memoir,” not flattened into settled biography. Allegations about plagiarism, fabricated research, fundraising, athletic feats, and the memoir's factual accuracy remain attributed and disputed; the article is not an adjudication.

The same feature also reports questions about dissertation text and research interviews, and quotes Liverpool John Moores University as saying that a confidential procedure was conducted and that Arday's PhD still stands. Those matters remain separate from the memoir's autobiographical source-version material and from any later institutional finding.
