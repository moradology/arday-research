# Caption audit

This source-version audits the Jason Arday segment of PBS’s *A World of Difference: Embracing Neurodiversity — The Importance of Early Intervention*. It preserves several subject-centred details that are not reduced to the recurring age-11/age-18 headline, while keeping PBS narration and Arday’s own testimony separate.

## Bounded passages

- **20:20–21:07** — Arday attributes his first word at eleven to hundreds or thousands of hours of speech therapy and his mother’s accompaniment. He says that speech development continued after “hello”: from roughly 11 to 16 he worked on phonics, soundings, word choice, tenses, phrases, and spoken dialogue, and only around 16 felt more comfortable with English vernacular. This is first-person developmental recollection, not a therapy or family record.
- **21:07–21:39** — Arday describes “living non-verbally” as a privilege in retrospect, saying it allowed him to observe and absorb the world and become intensely interested in human interaction. This is his interpretation of the experience, not an independent developmental conclusion.
- **21:42–22:22** — He says that when he entered university at 20 he had a reading age of 12, did not understand lectures or texts, and responded through repetition and practice. He reports writing 500 words every day for approximately 15 years and credits his mother with encouraging an unconventional route to achievement. These are self-reported practice and reading-age claims.
- **22:54–23:54** — Arday recounts asking what a PhD was, then telling Sandro Sandri about it; Sandri responds by imagining Arday earning a PhD within eight years, less than twelve years after he had begun reading and writing. The sequence is preserved as a first-person mentorship memory; the earlier unnamed lecturer and Sandri are not silently merged into one person.

PBS narration separately states the autism, speech/literacy chronology, Surrey training, degrees, and Cambridge superlatives. Those host formulations remain source-versioned and are not treated as independent corroboration. The official captions are retained for navigation; no wording is treated as a verbatim quotation until the MP4 is manually checked.
