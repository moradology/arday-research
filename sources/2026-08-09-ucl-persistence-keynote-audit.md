# *The Persistence of Racism in Higher Education*: page-level audit

## Object and event identity

The preserved 16-page landscape PDF is a PowerPoint-derived keynote deck titled *The Persistence of Racism in Higher Education: Understanding the BME Attainment Gap*. Its title slide names Dr Jason Arday, University College London, and the BME Attainment Conference on Tuesday 10 April 2018. PDF metadata names Jason Arday as author and gives a 10 May 2018 creation timestamp, which is a file-creation date rather than the event date.

This is a distinct 2018 keynote artifact. It is not the later UCL “Engaging pedagogically with race and racism in higher education” recording/16-slide deck, and it is not the 18 April 2018 UCL post-event report.

## Slide map

- **Slides 1–3:** title, critique of explanations that attribute the attainment gap to student-level variables, and the keynote’s three aims: conceptualise the BME attainment gap, examine institutional/structural challenges, and consider ways forward.
- **Slides 4–5:** contextual framing and a definition of the attainment gap. Slide 5 presents an ECU-attributed 2015/16 England comparison of 78.8% White versus 63.2% minority-ethnic qualifiers achieving a First or 2:1, a 15.6-point difference.
- **Slides 6–9:** structural, organisational, cultural, sensitivity, and institutional-identity challenges. The deck argues that deficit models, weak ownership, resistance, rhetoric, Eurocentric curricula, and staff representation matter.
- **Slides 10–12:** staff diversification and two anonymised quotations attributed to a Black female PhD English Literature participant and a Black male PhD History participant, drawn from the BME doctoral-student work cited in the deck.
- **Slides 13–15:** proposed changes and UCL leadership statements about the institution’s BME Attainment project and education strategy.
- **Slide 16:** references, including ECU, cited scholarship, the UCU BME doctoral-student report, and *Aiming Higher*.

## Evidence and argument boundary

The deck is an authored presentation of cited statistics, literature, public argument, and recommendations. It does not disclose a new keynote sample, survey, dataset, statistical reanalysis, intervention evaluation, or implementation results. The two participant quotations are presented as anonymised material from the cited doctoral-student work; they are not a new UCL study or independent corroboration.

Arday’s recommendations include diversifying academic staff, listening to BME student voice in curriculum design, recognising unconscious bias and burdens placed on BME women, and supporting inclusive curricula and institutional responsibility (slides 10 and 13). These are his dated pedagogical/policy positions, not measured causal findings.

## Source-version boundary

The slide’s 15.6-point figure and all other numerical/contextual claims should remain attributed to the cited ECU or other sources until those source records are checked directly. The deck supports a dated keynote/output edge and a map of Arday’s public framework; it does not independently validate its statistics, institutional claims, or predicted effects. Reopen for the original event recording/transcript, corrected deck, source-by-source statistic verification, or UCL implementation/evaluation records.
