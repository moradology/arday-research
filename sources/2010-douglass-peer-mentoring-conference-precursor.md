# Douglass/Smith peer-mentoring comparator

The public PDF is headed “Effective practices of peer mentors in an undergraduate writing intensive course” and names April G. Douglass and Dennie L. Smith of Texas A&M University. Inside the PDF it also carries the heading “An Exploration of the Characteristics of Effective Student Peer Mentoring Relationships.” Its text is a public source-version/precursor related to the published article by April G. Douglass, Dennie L. Smith and Lana J. Smith, *Mentoring & Tutoring* 21(2) (2013), pp. 219–234, DOI `10.1080/13611267.2013.813740`. The Taylor & Francis full-text request returned HTTP 403 on 2026-08-07, so the archive does not claim to possess the version of record.

## Direct comparison with Arday's thesis

Arday's thesis PDF pp. 308–310 contains a long passage beginning with peer-mentoring programmes as a source of “additional instructional support for students.” It continues with “additional instructional outlet,” relief of pressure from instructors/teacher trainers, adequate support/assistance, mentors reflecting on learning, the same Heirdsfield citation, emotional support, reduced intimidation, and students becoming “more comfortable with the material.” It then continues into writing-intensive courses and states that such courses help students learn subject matter and discipline-specific ways of thinking and writing, citing Douglass et al. (2013).

The public Douglass PDF pp. 1–2 contains the corresponding sequence in the same order: “additional instructional support for students,” “additional instructional outlet,” relieving pressure from course instructors, adequate opportunity for assistance, mentors/mentees reflecting on learning, the Heirdsfield citation, emotional support, reduced intimidation, becoming “more comfortable with the material,” and writing-intensive courses. The public precursor uses the same distinctive wording in multiple consecutive sentences and the same supporting-author cluster; Arday makes substitutions such as “student teachers” for “students,” “teacher trainers” for “course instructors,” and inserts additional citations and thesis-specific framing.

This is a substantially stronger direct textual-overlap observation than a shared general idea or common method. It still does not by itself establish intent, authorship history, permission, or an institutional plagiarism finding. The exact relationship between this public precursor and the 2013 version-of-record remains a source-version question because the publisher full text was inaccessible.
