# Page-level audit

The complete nine-page PESA Agora PDF was extracted with layout preservation and visually checked at the title/DOI page, opening argument, micro-aggressions section, conclusion, funding/contributor page, and references. It is a public full-text manifestation of Jason Arday’s DOI-identified article, not a second publication.

## Article structure and evidence boundary

- **Identity and type — PDF pp. 1–2:** the file identifies Jason Arday as sole author, gives DOI `10.1080/00131857.2020.1777640`, online publication date 25 June 2020, and labels the text “EDITORIAL.” The article does not present an abstract, participant sample, interview protocol, questionnaire, statistical model, or original results section.
- **Opening evidence — PDF pp. 2–3:** the article cites Leading Routes, HESA, UKRI, AdvanceHE, and other literature while discussing doctoral access, BAME representation, Eurocentric curricula, staff progression, and professorial disparities. Its headline figures—including the cited 15,560 first-year UK-domiciled PhD students, 3% Black share, and 245 of 19,868 UKRI-funded studentships—are reported source claims, not a dataset generated or reanalysed in this article.
- **Policy/intervention argument — PDF pp. 3–4:** Arday argues that equality policies can become weak, image-oriented, or insufficiently evaluated, and discusses AdvanceHE’s Race Equality Charter as a framework whose long-term potency remained under evaluation at the article date. These are editorial analyses and normative claims, not an evaluation of a named institution.
- **Micro-aggressions and Whiteness — PDF pp. 4–5:** the article synthesizes cited scholarship on institutional racism, micro-aggressions, colourblindness, Whiteness, double consciousness, and mental-health effects. It presents no new participant testimony or original measurement; the passage is a literature-based argument.
- **Conclusion — PDF pp. 5–6:** recommendations include targeted recruitment and progression support, funded studentships, reciprocal mentoring, communities of practice, career networks, institutional accountability, and collaboration with public-facing equality organisations. No recommendation is implemented or evaluated in the article.
- **Dated biography and funding — PDF pp. 6–8:** the article states UCU funding, no conflict of interest, and a 2020 contributor biography listing Durham, Ohio State, Nelson Mandela University, Runnymede, BSA, CLASS, Shaftesbury Park, TUC/UCU, UKCGE, Universities UK, editorial boards, and named books. These are publication-time biography claims; they are not current employment, legal-register, or independent publication records.
- **Terminology — PDF p. 6:** the note defines the article’s use of BAME, acknowledges the term’s limitations, and says it was applied descriptively and in line with participant preference. This is an explicit terminology choice, not evidence that the category is methodologically unproblematic.

## Interpretation boundary

The article is strong evidence of Arday’s 2020 public scholarly argument and of the external sources he selected to support it. It should not be represented as an empirical study of BME doctoral students, as an independent verification of the cited figures, or as proof that its policy recommendations worked. The PESA PDF is a mirror manifestation; no byte identity with the Taylor & Francis version-of-record is claimed.
