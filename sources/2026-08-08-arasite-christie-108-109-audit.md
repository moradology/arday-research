# Christie comparison: Arday pp. 108–109

## Scope and materials

The `Aderibigbe-Christie-Le Cornu.docx` table in the preserved `plag2.zip` maps Arday thesis p. 108 to Hazel Christie’s 2014 article p. 955 and Arday p. 109 to Christie p. 959. The table’s “verbatim” and related labels are dossier-author characterizations, not institutional findings. The Edinburgh repository PDF is a public article manifestation of Christie’s *Teaching in Higher Education* article, volume 19(8), pp. 955–965, DOI `10.1080/13562517.2014.934355`.

## Direct comparison

Arday p. 108 begins by attributing to Christie the emergence of new perspectives on learning over the previous three decades and the recognition that learning is social rather than individual. It then follows Christie’s sequence through situated learning, institutional environments, participation and engagement, and the social practices that support successful learners. Arday changes or adds wording—most notably “student teachers,” “reciprocal methods,” equality/power language, and additional citations—but retains a long ordered passage and the same Anderson–McCune, Lave–Wenger, Brockbank–McGill, and Falchikov reference cluster. The Christie citation is present in Arday’s paragraph, but the captured passage does not use quotation marks around the retained wording.

The same Arday page also develops power and control in mentoring, while the next page returns to the effectiveness of mentor training and the roles of participants. Arday p. 109 preserves Christie’s distinctive agency-versus-passivity formulation: who is active and sets the agenda, versus who is a passive recipient of the scheme. Arday changes the surrounding attribution and adds discussion of student teachers, peer mentoring, power distribution, and Ponte/Twomey, but the distinctive sentence and conceptual placement remain closely aligned with Christie’s p. 959.

The relationship is therefore stronger than a shared topic or generic mentoring vocabulary, but it is not accurately described as an uninterrupted word-for-word reproduction of the entire mapped section. The local comparison preserves both the close wording and the edits rather than adopting the exhibit’s labels wholesale.

## Interpretation boundary

This is a reproducible page-level textual and structural relationship in two public primary texts. It does not determine whether Arday used Christie’s article directly, used an intermediate source, whether the citation was intended to cover the retained language, whether quotation conventions were satisfied, or whether any institution found plagiarism or misconduct. The comparison is distinct from the Aderibigbe and Zwozdiak-Myers audits and does not imply coverage of other `plag2` rows.
