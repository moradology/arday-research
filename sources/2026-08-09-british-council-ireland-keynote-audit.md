# Scope

This audit treats the recovered 14-page PDF as a presentation artifact. It separates what the slides visibly say from the external sources they cite, and from claims about delivery, later implementation, or the accuracy of the cited statistics. It is deduplicated to [SRC-584](2019-01-30-british-council-ireland-keynote.md) and the related 2020 event record [SRC-586](2020-01-30-british-council-ria-dialogue.md).

## Artifact identity and chronology

- The title slide names Dr Jason Arday, the British Council, and the Royal Irish Academy Ireland, and gives the title “How can and should University leadership deliver greater inclusion?”
- The visible title-slide date is Thursday 30 January 2019.
- Slides 3–4 cite a 2020 Guardian article and 2019 HESA figures. The source filename also contains “jan 2020.” These are incompatible with a straightforward January 2019 delivery date. The record therefore preserves the slide-visible date as an artifact feature, not as a resolved event date.

## Page-level content map

- pp. 2–5: racial inequality framing; senior-management representation; HESA-attributed counts; and a critique of rounded/aggregated workforce figures.
- pp. 6–8: a Guardian quotation attributed to Chris Skidmore; HESA-attributed non-academic managerial counts; and BBC-FOI-attributed pay figures for Russell Group universities.
- pp. 9–11: the rationale for diverse leadership; a structural-inequality argument; and an unattributed quotation from a Black female university senior leader.
- pp. 12–14: recommendations on organizational culture, mentoring, targeted support, affirmative-action shortlisting, selection participation, leadership training/networking, and a concluding Style Council quotation.

## Evidence boundaries

The HESA, BBC, Guardian, and scholarly citations are preserved as citations made by the deck; their underlying datasets and publication chronology were not independently reaudited here. The senior-leader quotation is not identified by name or source in the deck. Recommendations are Arday’s presented policy position, not evidence of adoption or effect. The PDF establishes a public presentation artifact and its contents, not that the talk was delivered on the visible date, that every statistic is correct, or that the proposed interventions worked.
