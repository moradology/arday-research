# Page-level audit

The preserved LJMU repository PDF is 401 pages. It is titled “An exploration of peer-mentoring among student teachers’ to inform reflective practice within action research,” names Jason A. K. Arday as author, and states that it was submitted for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy at Liverpool John Moores University in July 2015. This audit distinguishes the thesis’s documented design from its author-reported interpretations and conclusions.

## What the thesis reports

- **pp. 1–2:** the abstract frames the study as a qualitative investigation of peer-mentoring and reflective practice among student teachers. It reports a four-phase sequential data-collection strategy using interviews, reflective pro-formas, workshops, and open-ended questionnaires; the intervention sample is four student teachers selected purposively. The stated findings concern reciprocal mentoring, professional development, and challenges involving trust, power, and time.
- **pp. 140–146:** the thesis says ethical approval was granted by the LJMU Research Ethics Committee under LJMU and British Educational Research Association guidance. It describes a qualitative interpretive case study, a concurrent/triangulated design, and two research questions about peer-mentoring, collaborative learning, reflective practice, and practical challenges. The four phases comprise needs analysis; twelve student-teacher-led workshops; reflective practice; and evaluation/mapping/alignment.
- **pp. 147–153:** an open invitation went to 46 final-year undergraduate physical-education students at a university in North-West England; 24 questionnaires were returned. The thesis says the eventual intervention participants had to secure a full-time initial teacher-training place. It reports that 14 of the 24 applied to the recruiting university and that ten external applicants were unsuccessful, leaving four intervention participants.
- **pp. 149–150:** the thesis explicitly says no comparative analysis was undertaken between the 24 questionnaire respondents and the final four intervention participants. It also notes that nearly two-thirds of the initial interest came from mature students, while not presenting a comparative analysis of that observation.
- **pp. 159–170:** the intervention is described as twelve themed workshops, generally every two or three weeks for 60–90 minutes from September 2013 to July 2014, with weekly peer-mentoring meetings and reflective pro-formas. Workshop topics could change by collective agreement in response to participants’ reported needs.
- **pp. 174–188:** Arday discusses researcher positionality, power, possible influence, reliability, validity, and inference quality. He acknowledges a possible conflict because of a previous lecturer affiliation with some undergraduate students, says participants could withdraw, and describes four analysis stages: reflective data collection, transcription, understanding the data, and theme development.
- **pp. 190–198:** the thesis reports post-intervention follow-up with the four participants and describes perceived continuing use of reflective and collegial practices. It states that three of the four had completed their newly qualified teacher year and one had taken a two-year sabbatical after the PGCE at the time of writing.
- **pp. 200–285:** the findings chapter presents qualitative narratives and themes around professional learning support, mentoring relationships, reflective teaching, communities of practice, trust, time, hierarchy, and the perceived impact of the intervention. The reported data are participants’ accounts and workshop/questionnaire materials, not a controlled comparison of attainment or teaching outcomes.
- **pp. 330–352:** the conclusion recommends collaborative, reciprocal mentoring and further research. The limitations section says the study cannot establish with certainty that development of reflective practice was caused by the intervention and identifies narrative-analysis misinterpretation, researcher subjectivity/bias, and the small purposive sample as limitations.

## Evidence boundary

The thesis is strong primary evidence for what Arday submitted, the research questions he chose, the methods he says he used, and the claims he made in 2015. It is not independent verification that every procedure occurred as described, nor does the public PDF expose the underlying recordings, questionnaires, raw coding, ethics file, or participant-level dataset. The four-person intervention sample, self-selection and eligibility funnel, lack of respondent-to-intervention comparison, and author-reported qualitative interpretation limit generalisation and causal inference. Those limits are stated by the thesis itself and should remain attached to any summary of its findings.

This audit does not adjudicate later allegations about other publications. It records a separate primary artifact and preserves the thesis’s own methodological and epistemological claims for comparison with later work.
