# The Guardian: Cambridge academics reject university’s attempt to end row over Jason Arday

Richard Adams’s 7 August 2026 report describes the institutional response after Arday’s resignation. It quotes Cambridge saying that its investigation into the circumstances of Arday’s appointment and tenure will continue, that findings will feed into a review of senior-academic appointments, and that the Research Policy Committee will review the misconduct-in-research policy.

The report also describes a letter to Vice-Chancellor Deborah Prentice signed by nearly 100 Cambridge academics. The signatories called for an independent body of senior academics from inside and outside Cambridge, a clear completion timetable, and a commitment to share findings with the university and public. Their requested scope included the appointment process, risk assessment and media narrative, handling of academic-misconduct allegations, and whether the university was involved in efforts to deter journalism. These are the signatories’ demands, not evidence that those events occurred or that Cambridge accepted the proposed mechanism.

The article reports that Jesus College ended its parallel process after stating that Arday’s fellowship was contingent on his university appointment and that acceptance of his resignation concluded the process announced the previous evening. This is a reported college statement, distinct from the university investigation and from the historical 2025–26 fellows roster.

## Evidence boundary

This article is a dated report of public institutional wording and an internal letter. It does not provide the letter’s complete signatory list, the underlying risk assessment, a Cambridge case file, a final investigation, or the proposed independent body’s findings.
