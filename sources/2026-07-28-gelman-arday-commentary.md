# Andrew Gelman — Cambridge controversy commentary

Andrew Gelman’s 28 July 2026 post in *The Future of Statistical Modeling* is a distinct statistical/commentary response to the Arday controversy. Gelman describes the public story as having been called a plagiarism scandal but frames the central allegation as data fabrication, with plagiarism as a means rather than the entire issue. He discusses the duplicated or highly similar participant quotation reported in the public materials and argues that the question cannot be resolved by text comparison alone: the relevant recordings, transcripts, and consent materials are not public in the captured record.

The post also repeats and evaluates claims about the 2018 correction, the 2021 correction, the Cofnas/Harris materials, Cambridge’s response, and the alleged role of Carter-Ruck. Its judgments about Arday, Cambridge staff, and the spokesperson are Gelman’s rhetoric and should not be promoted to verified facts.

## Evidence boundary

This source is useful as an independent, technically informed framing of the distinction between textual overlap and possible fabrication of empirical evidence. It does not itself inspect the underlying participant records, establish that data were fabricated, or supply an institutional finding. The underlying articles, corrections, comparison materials, and institutional statements remain canonical separately.
