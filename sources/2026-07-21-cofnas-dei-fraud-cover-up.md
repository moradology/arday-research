# Nathan Cofnas: “DEI Fraud and Cover-Up at Cambridge”

Nathan Cofnas's 21 July 2026 article presents a polemical critique of Jason Arday's scholarship and biography. Its political framing and conclusions are not adopted as facts. The article is nevertheless a material research lead because it links the underlying 2015 LJMU thesis and 2009 Brunel thesis, displays side-by-side text comparisons, identifies further source texts, and points to journal corrections.

## Dissertation examples

Cofnas says he compared Arday's 2015 thesis with Paula Zwozdiak-Myers's 2009 thesis using Copyleaks. The displayed examples show long passages with matching ordering, syntax, and distinctive wording, sometimes with small substitutions or stylistic edits. He also identifies two further alleged source texts—Hussain et al. (2011) and Kahn et al. (2008)—and says they are not cited in Arday's dissertation.

The archive treats this as a serious textual-overlap allegation, not as a final finding. SRC-209 independently preserves the underlying theses, Cofnas-linked material, a public 185-pair appendix, and a separate reproducibility analysis reporting 179 pairs with at least 12 consecutive identical words, 107 blocks, and a longest shared run of 63 words. That analysis also warns that it cannot establish intent.

## Publication examples

Cofnas discusses the 2018 *Social Sciences* mental-health paper and quotes its later correction, which added citations to Memon et al. (2016) and Grey et al. (2013) across the abstract, introduction, methods, results/discussion, and conclusions. He also discusses the 2021 *Educational Philosophy and Theory* correction clarifying the relationship between that paper, Arday's 2018 study, and Memon et al. (2016).

The article further reports alleged overlap between interview material in Arday's 2018 and 2022 papers, including a quote that appears in accounts attributed to different participants, and alleged recycling of a methods paragraph in the 2022 precarious-employment paper. These are especially important because they raise questions beyond unattributed prose: whether source material, interview data, or participant quotations were reused or misrepresented. The underlying papers and correction records must be compared directly before treating these as established findings.

The archive has now directly compared the related 2018 and 2021 article PDFs. An extended quotation about improving English, difficulty articulating concerns to university healthcare professionals, reluctance to discuss psychological issues, and fear of being heavily medicated appears in both papers, but the participant descriptors differ: “Female, Asian, UG, 4” in the 2018 article and “Female, Latin-American, Female, Academic, 38” in the 2021 article. This is a verified textual relationship and a material provenance question; it remains distinct from proof of fabricated participants or a research-integrity finding. The 2016 Memon article cited by the correction is now preserved as SRC-382. The 2022 precarious-employment methods allegation remains unadjudicated and is not treated as equivalent without a fuller text comparison.

Cofnas also characterizes several qualitative, opinion, protocol, and autoethnographic papers as methodologically weak because they do not use statistical analysis. That criticism is not accepted wholesale: qualitative and autoethnographic research does not require quantitative statistics. The stronger evidence is the specific textual overlap, citation correction, and possible quotation/data-reuse claims—not the absence of statistics by itself.

## Evidence boundary

Cofnas has a documented political agenda and uses highly charged language. That affects how his conclusions should be weighed, but it does not make the underlying side-by-side comparisons irrelevant. The proper next step is direct comparison of the cited primary texts, followed by independent assessment of quotation provenance, citation placement, ethics statements, methods, and author contributions. Cambridge and LJMU statements remain separate institutional evidence tracks; no public comprehensive adjudication is preserved here.
