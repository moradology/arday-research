# Page-level audit

The complete 13-page public mirror PDF was extracted with layout preservation and reviewed against rendered pages for the executive summary, methods/sample, themes, conclusion/recommendations, and references. The report is dated June 2017 and is attributed to Jason Arday. It is a commissioned qualitative report, not a journal article or population survey.

## Version and page map

- **Version boundary — PDF pp. 1–13:** the Pih mirror exposes a 13-page Word-derived PDF. The UCU-hosted browser-visible manifestation exposed 11 pages during the earlier capture and its shell download route returned access-boundary HTML. The two manifestations are retained separately; byte identity is not claimed.
- **Purpose and context — PDF pp. 1–5:** the report frames BME doctoral students’ perceptions of academic careers within wider higher-education representation, progression, salary, and professorial disparities. The sector figures are attributed to ECU, HESA, HEFCE, Leading Routes, and other cited sources; the report does not generate a new administrative dataset.
- **Methods and sample — PDF pp. 5–6:** the report says 20 BME doctoral research students from 16 UK universities participated. It describes a predominantly arts-and-humanities sample, majority female and mainly UK-national, with a semi-structured questionnaire followed by a semi-structured interview between January and March 2016. The table gives 4 male/16 female; Asian 2, Black 8, Black White and Mixed Heritage 7, Other 3; 14 UK and 6 non-UK; and age groups of 10 at 30 or under, 5 aged 31–40, and 5 aged 41–55.
- **Career appetite and support — PDF pp. 6–8:** all but one respondent reportedly wanted an academic role. The report organizes material around the attractiveness of teaching/research, supervisory and mentoring support, informal networks, isolation, micro-aggressions, and the usefulness or absence of senior BME support. Quotations are anonymized respondent material as presented by the report.
- **Marginalisation, typecasting, and teaching — PDF pp. 8–10:** the report records accounts of being mistaken for cleaners or students, exclusion from informal networks, “race person” typecasting, difficulty accessing publication/funding/collaboration opportunities, and uncertainty around teaching opportunities and tokenism. These are reported participant accounts and author synthesis, not independently adjudicated incidents.
- **Conclusion and recommendations — PDF pp. 11–12:** the report explicitly says the small sample is not generalisable to the wider BME student population. It recommends ethnicity-sensitive attrition research, sector-wide supervision/mentoring standards, senior-mentor systems, bias and isolation training, support for BME networks, transparent development access, targeted national funding, and research-council attention to underrepresentation.
- **References and funding — PDF pp. 12–13:** the report lists external literature and statistics and identifies UCU funding. It does not expose raw questionnaires, interview transcripts, recruitment records, coding files, ethics documentation, response rates, or a participant-level dataset.

## Interpretation boundary

The report is strong evidence for what Arday’s 2017 commissioned research claimed to study, how the disclosed sample was structured, what themes and recommendations it presented, and how he publicly framed the issue at that date. It is not evidence that the cited national statistics were independently reanalysed by him, that every reported incident occurred as described, or that the recommendations were implemented or effective. The report should remain linked to the later *Fighting the tide* editorial and other outputs as part of an output lineage, not treated as independent corroboration of their claims.
