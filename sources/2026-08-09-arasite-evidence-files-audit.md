# Arasite evidence files: provenance and scope audit

## Object and provenance

The preserved `plagmenu.html` index presents four ZIP archives as a coordinated set of evidence files and says they were submitted to the Cambridge and Liverpool John Moores research-integrity offices in September 2025. The page attributes compilation and checking to named/unnamed contributors, but the archive contains no institutional receipt, terms of reference, decision, or response. The submission statement is therefore a claim made by the public host, not an independently verified institutional fact.

Each ZIP contains one DOCX comparison file. The ZIP directory listings show internal timestamps on 27 July 2026:

- `plag1.zip`: *More Z-M - summaries(1).docx*, comparing Arday thesis extracts with Zwozdiak-Myers material.
- `plag2.zip`: *Aderibigbe-Christie-Le Cornu.docx*, comparing Arday extracts with Aderibigbe, Christie, and Le Cornu material.
- `plag3.zip`: *Arday - Texas A&M University source.docx*, comparing Arday extracts with Douglass, Smith & Smith (2013).
- `plag4.zip`: *ARDAY FINAL GRID.docx*, a larger Arday/Zwozdiak-Myers grid.

The apparent gap between the index’s September 2025 submission description and the DOCX/ZIP timestamps dated July 2026 is preserved as a source-version discrepancy. The public page does not say whether the files were revised after submission.

## What the artifacts contain

The extracted tables preserve source-labelled page references, excerpts from Arday’s 2015 thesis, comparator excerpts, a classification column, and notes. The labels include `Verbatim`, `Near-verbatim`, `Close paraphrase`, and related formulations. These are the dossier authors’ analytical classifications. They are not plagiarism determinations, institutional findings, or peer-reviewed results.

The four files are not four independent witnesses. They are one coordinated dossier family hosted by the same site, presented through one index, and explicitly described as complementary materials. The existing page-level audits of particular grid segments remain the controlling records for any bounded primary-text comparison; this audit does not re-count those rows.

## Missing and broken materials

The index links a relation diagram whose HTML points to a publisher-local `C:/Users/darri/.../image.jpeg` path. No image binary was recovered. The dossier also does not expose the alleged institutional submissions, correspondence, investigator reports, raw comparison code, or a complete chain of custody beyond the public files and host statements.

## Evidentiary boundary

The files are valuable leads because they make specific passages and page references inspectable. They support claims about what the dossier contains and which textual relationships its authors allege. They do not, by themselves, establish that any passage was plagiarised, that an applicable academic standard was breached, that Arday intended to mislead, or that any institution reached a particular conclusion. Those questions require checking each cited passage against the original texts, considering quotation and citation context and possible intermediate sources, and obtaining authoritative process records.

The dossier remains linked to the existing integrity ontology as an allegation/comparison source, not as a `fraud`, `liar`, or `villain` finding. Reopen for a later dossier version, the missing relation-diagram image, institutional receipt/response, original-source comparison, or formal outcome.
