# *The Runnymede School Report*: page-level audit

## Object and manifestation

The report’s front matter identifies *The Runnymede School Report: Race, Education and Inequality in Contemporary Britain*, ISBN `978-1-909546-10-3`, published by Runnymede in August 2015 and edited by Claire Alexander, Debbie Weekes-Bernard, and Jason Arday. The recovered Manchester PDF is 61 physical pages: the report itself runs through printed p. 56, followed by five pages of Manchester Research Explorer metadata and rights text. Use printed report pages for substantive citations and retain the appended wrapper as a repository-manifestation boundary.

## Page map and authorship

- **Printed pp. 3–5:** foreword by Debbie Abrahams MP and the co-authored introduction, “Race and Education – Contemporary Contexts and Challenges,” credited to Alexander, Weekes-Bernard, and Arday. The introduction discusses racial inequality, curriculum, Britishness, attainment, school structures, and the policy context; it is a collective editorial contribution, not an Arday-only argument.
- **Printed pp. 6–47 and 51–55:** chapters by other named contributors on ideologies, attainment, teacher education, school cultures, Islamophobia, social mixing, and Black History. Their findings and arguments are not attributed to Arday merely because he co-edited the volume.
- **Printed pp. 48–50:** Arday’s chapter, “Considering Mentoring among BME Learners and Issues Concerning Teacher Training: The Narratives of Students and Teachers,” credited to Jason Arday, Leeds Beckett University.
- **Printed p. 56:** contributor biographies; physical pp. 57–61 are the repository wrapper rather than report content.

## Arday chapter: method and material

The chapter opens with a quotation attributed to a Year 11 student and says it comes from interviews in a research project exploring the views of 14–18-year-old secondary and sixth-form students. It states that the project drew on the narratives of five female students, ten male students, three secondary-school teachers, and one sixth-form teacher (printed p. 48). The chapter discusses mentoring, teacher expectations, stereotyping, culturally aware teacher education, and racial inequality in school contexts.

The chapter presents participant quotations and the author’s synthesis of themes, including racial stereotyping, teacher preparedness, self-efficacy, mentoring, and the need for cultural-awareness training. It recommends that teacher-training courses embed assessment on ethnic/racial diversity and that schools provide staff-development workshops on race, cultural awareness, and diversity (printed pp. 49–50).

The preserved chapter does not disclose recruitment details, response rate, interview protocol, ethics approval, coding procedure, transcripts, or a participant-level dataset. Its sample and themes therefore remain bounded qualitative evidence from the chapter’s disclosed project, not a representative account of BME learners or proof that the recommendations caused improved outcomes.

## Interpretation boundary

This artifact establishes Arday’s 2015 co-editorial role, a co-authored introduction, and a distinct authored chapter with a disclosed small interview-based project. It does not make him author of the other chapters, and it does not independently verify the participant accounts or the external statistics and literature cited throughout the collection. The appended repository pages must not be mistaken for additional report content or a second publication.
