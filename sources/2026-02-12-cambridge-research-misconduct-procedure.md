# University of Cambridge research-misconduct procedure (2026)

Cambridge’s 22-page procedure defines the institution’s stated process and scope. Section 2.1 applies it to allegations of Research Misconduct against people undertaking research as members of or connected to a University institution or using University facilities or funding. Section 2.2 says the University may consider allegations relating to research carried out while a person was associated with Cambridge, including former students, subject to seriousness, credibility, and the prospects of investigating fairly.

Section 3 expressly includes fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, misrepresentation of data, involvement/authorship, qualifications/experience/credentials, publication history, and improper dealing with allegations. Section 7 separates three stages: initial screening, preliminary investigation, and formal investigation. Section 8.4 says initial screening asks whether a complaint falls within scope, meets the Research Misconduct definition, and is not trivial or clearly without foundation; section 8.7 permits dismissal or referral to another procedure if those requirements are not met.

## Evidence handling

This policy is the primary interpretive context for the phrase “out of scope” reported by SRC-402. It shows that “out of scope” is a procedural screening category, not equivalent by itself to a finding that allegations were false, that no textual overlap existed, or that no misconduct occurred. It does not reveal which stage, if any, Cambridge applied to the Arday matter, what evidence it considered, or what decision was made. No case-specific inference is drawn.
