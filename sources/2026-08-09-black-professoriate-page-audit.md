# Page audit

This is a page-level audit of Jason Arday’s six-page article in *On Education: Journal for Research and Debate*, volume 5, issue 13 (April 2022). It is a new audit record, not a new publication node: the article remains canonical under [SRC-041](2022-black-professoriate.md), with journal DOI `10.17899/on_ed.2022.13.4` and repository DOI `10.25656/01:29933`.

## Article content and evidence boundary

- **Abstract and purpose — PDF p. 1:** Arday describes the article as a “succinct synthesis of recent evidence” about how the UK academy marginalizes Black academics from the professoriate. Its stated aim is to identify the problem and press universities and policymakers toward progression and equality. The article does not present a new participant sample, questionnaire, interview protocol, dataset, statistical model, or original results section.
- **Reported sector figures — PDF pp. 1–2:** the article reports HESA January 2022 data as 160 Black professors out of 22,855 UK professors, and reports 2.8% of higher-education staff identifying as Black against 3.3% of the general UK population. It also says BME staff are less likely to be on permanent contracts, in senior management, in professorial roles, or on higher salary bands, citing Arday et al. (2022). These are dated article claims and cited-source summaries, not an independent reanalysis performed here.
- **Mental-health argument — PDF p. 2:** the article connects racial stereotyping, undermining, exclusion, and marginalization with fatigue, psychological distress, and mental-health effects for Black academics. It cites earlier literature and Arday’s 2021 work; the passage is a synthesis and normative argument rather than a new clinical study.
- **Black women — PDF pp. 2–3:** the article reports 38 Black female professors and contrasts that figure with the statement that 23% of the professoriate were white women. It frames Black women’s progression through intersectional inequality and resistance strategies. The article’s local text preserves the figures and citations, but this audit does not independently reconstruct the underlying denominator or source dataset.
- **Recommendations — PDF pp. 3–5:** the conclusion calls for institutional transparency about progression, attention to gatekeeping and marginalization, mentoring and nurturing of aspiring Black academics, and a more substantive approach to diversity than shallow inclusion. It lists five sector challenges involving homogeneity assumptions, intersectional experience, mentoring, whiteness/power, and organizational adaptability.
- **Publication-time biography — PDF p. 6:** the author note describes Arday as Professor of Sociology of Education at Glasgow, formerly Associate Professor at Durham and Deputy Executive Dean for People and Culture, and lists visiting/fellowship, trustee, advisory, and RSA roles. This is dated journal biography copy from April 2022; it is not current employment or legal-register evidence. Several role descriptions require separate reconciliation against later host and regulator records.

## Interpretation boundary

The article is useful evidence of Arday’s published public-intellectual position and of the sources/figures he chose to synthesize in 2022. It should not be represented as an empirical study conducted by him, nor should its cited statistics be silently upgraded to independently verified facts. The final-page biography is preserved as a source-version and does not resolve later affiliation or trusteeship changes.
