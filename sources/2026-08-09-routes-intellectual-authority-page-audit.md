# Scope

This audit reads the 29-page Queen’s University Belfast publisher/version-of-record PDF for Belluigi, Arday, and O’Keeffe, “The routes to intellectual authority in a prior colonial empire.” It extracts the article’s stated method, data boundary, selected tables/findings, and conclusion. The paper’s results and interpretations remain claims made by the authors from the HESA data they obtained; this audit is not an independent reanalysis.

## Page-level map

- **pp. 303–305:** the article frames full-professor/chair positions as routes to intellectual authority and states that it contributes a critical quantitative analysis of HESA administrative data on academic staff in UK public universities, with particular attention to Education.
- **pp. 308–309 — method/data boundary:** the authors say access was purchased to HESA data covering 24,530 academic staff. They explicitly discuss restrictions in the dataset, category construction, missingness, and the problem of treating administrative data as authoritative or “unbiased.” They situate their reading in Quantitative Critical Race Theory and describe using quantitative data as anti-oppressive praxis.
- **pp. 311–312 — composition:** the article reports that, in its 2020 data, 75% of UK university staff were recorded as white, 16.5% as racialised minorities, and 9% unknown; in Education, it reports 69% white, 8% racialised minorities, and 6.5% unknown. It compares Education’s composition with population records and reports lower representation of racialised minorities in Education.
- **pp. 315–317 — progression and leadership:** the article reports that racialised minorities were concentrated at junior levels and that, in Education, it observed no promotions of “black staff” to full professor during the period studied. Its tables report differentiated proportions for full professors and senior management and identify absent or near-absent categories for some Black and Bangladeshi groups.
- **pp. 321–323 — synthesis:** the authors treat Education as a case through which to examine disciplinary cultures, argue that conditions were less favourable for racialised staff, and foreground the intersection of race, gender, nationality/geopolitics, and academic hierarchy. The conclusion says administrative-data categorisation, collection, and reporting can obscure accountability.

## Evidence boundary

The article is a co-authored scholarly output and a primary record of the authors’ analysis and argument. The reported percentages, categories, and interpretations should not be silently upgraded into an independent statistical finding without the underlying HESA extracts, code, and replication materials. The audit does not attribute the whole article to Arday alone or infer his individual contribution from co-authorship.
