# 2018 article audit

## Identity and version

The locally preserved 16-page accepted author manuscript identifies Jason Arday as author and the University of Roehampton School of Education as affiliation on the title page. The article is titled “Understanding Race and Educational Leadership in Higher Education: Exploring the Black and Ethnic Minority (BME) Experience” and is the manuscript version of the 2018 *Management in Education* article, DOI `10.1177/0892020618791002`, pp. 192–200. The repository warns that the accepted manuscript may differ from the version of record; this audit does not claim byte identity with the publisher PDF.

## Page-level content map

- **PDF pp. 2–7 — abstract and framing.** The abstract says the article uses collective biography to work with narratives from three BME academics in senior higher-education leadership. The introduction frames under-representation, racialized organizational cultures, leadership access, and cited sector statistics. These are the article’s literature/framing claims, not independently reanalysed statistics.
- **PDF pp. 8–9 — method and participant material.** The method describes a purposeful sample of three BME senior leaders from three UK higher-education institutions, collective biographical reflection, a focus-group/cathartic process, and three guiding questions about racism, support for leadership progression, and the continuing scarcity of BME senior leaders. The article does not name the participants or identify the institutions in this manuscript.
- **PDF pp. 9–11 — narrative findings.** Three anonymized participant voices are labelled only by gender, racial identity, and senior-leader status. The discussion groups their accounts around scrutiny, undermined authority, microaggressions, isolation, vulnerability, and constrained leadership pathways. These are reported participant narratives and the author’s synthesis, not prevalence estimates or an independently verified institutional dataset.
- **PDF pp. 11–12 — conclusion and recommendations.** The conclusion recommends targeted mentoring, affirmative-action/shortlisting interventions, participation of BME academics in selection, leadership development, communities of practice, and compulsory equality-and-diversity training. The recommendations are normative/policy proposals; this article does not report an intervention evaluation or causal test of their effectiveness.
- **PDF pp. 12–16 — references.** The manuscript’s bibliography includes the cited literature and policy sources; no raw interview transcripts, coding scheme, consent materials, or supplementary dataset is appended.

## Method boundary

The article is a small qualitative/collective-biography account. Its own disclosed design supports close interpretation of three participants’ experiences; it does not support generalizing the quoted experiences to all BME academics or treating the quoted claims about university racism as independently measured findings. The audit records the absence of participant names, institution names, raw materials, and an explicit outcome evaluation in the preserved manuscript without inferring that such materials never existed.
