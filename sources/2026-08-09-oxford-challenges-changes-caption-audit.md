# Caption audit

This source-version audits selected Arday-attributed passages in Oxford Physics’s 56:08 *Challenges & Changes* recording. The opening introduction is host speech and is not used for substantive claims. The audit focuses on Arday’s presentation of research and his stated evidentiary position.

## Bounded passages

- **00:04:20–00:06:30** — Arday presents a pipeline account of Black and minority-ethnic participation in higher education and doctoral funding. He says the work was led by Paulette Williams of Leading Routes and that he participated with Chantelle Lewis and Sukhi Bath; he describes presenting the research to the Office for Students and encountering reluctance to accept the implications. The underlying *Broken Pipeline* report is a separate primary record and controls its own figures and methodology.
- **00:04:35–00:06:30** — He cites figures including roughly 30,000 PhD studentships and 30 awarded to Black Caribbean students, and describes a wider structural shortage of Black professors. These are statistics as presented in the talk; the captions are imperfect, and the figures should be checked against the underlying HESA/UKRI-attributed sources rather than treated as independently verified by the recording.
- **00:06:40–00:08:10** — He describes a 2018 study with the BBC on ethnic pay differentials in the Russell Group, reporting average salaries of £52,000 for white academics, £38,000 for Black academics, and £32,000 for Arab academics. He uses the figures to argue that qualification and capability do not by themselves remove structural disadvantage. The talk does not supply the study’s dataset or full method.
- **00:08:35–00:10:20** — Arday says that a person of colour’s account of experiencing racism should not be neutralized or challenged simply because it lacks a statistic. He criticizes “colour-blind” and “race card” framings and argues that structural racism can be understood through lived experience as well as quantitative evidence. This is a dated epistemic and public-policy position, not a general methodological rule established by the recording.
