# Social Science Space interview — Arday-side audit

This audit focuses on Jason Arday’s role and words in the canonical Sage *Social Science Space* interview with Harshad Keval. It does not audit Keval’s book or silently convert Keval’s answers into Arday claims. The publisher transcript uses speaker labels; the local YouTube captions remain a derivative layer with recognition errors.

## Bounded Arday passages

- **00:00–00:50** — Arday introduces himself as working in race and intersectional areas, describes Keval as a close friend, and frames the conversation as a discussion of Keval’s work. This is self-description and relationship framing.
- **06:40–08:20** — He asks about the title and the concept of “white narcissism,” explicitly noting that he had not previously encountered the term and crediting Keval with its development. This records interviewer framing and epistemic attribution, not Arday’s authorship of the concept.
- **09:00–13:30** — He asks how much of Keval’s book reflects personal experience versus wider experiences of ethnic-minority people in higher education. The questions frame racial inequality and discrimination as the context of the conversation; they are not independent evidence about sector prevalence.
- **14:00–18:30** — He asks how anti-racist work and the book have been received amid political turbulence, attacks on egalitarian ideals, a perceived rightward shift, and increasing precarity for people producing this work. This is a dated public framing of the issue.
- **20:20–23:20** — He asks Keval to explain compassion in the academy and calls him one of the most compassionate people he has encountered in the sector. The latter is Arday’s evaluative opinion, not an external accolade or measured trait.
- **24:20–26:50** — He asks for three take-home messages, says the book can speak to varied experiences of race and intersection, and closes by calling it a contribution to knowledge and a movement requiring continued organisation against racism. These are editorial/praise statements and public-position framing.

## Evidence boundary

The publisher’s transcript is the strongest text for speaker attribution in this record. It supports what Arday says as interviewer and his identification as one of two editors of Sage’s *Social Science for Social Justice* series. It does not independently establish his employment, friendship history, the truth of the social diagnosis in his questions, or Keval’s answers. The recording date is not stated; 9 April 2025 is the publication date, not a claimed recording date.
