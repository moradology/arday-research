## Comparison

Cofnas's displayed comparison places Arday thesis p. 78 beside Kahn et al.'s discussion of Edwards/Nicoll and Fanghanel. Arday follows the same ordered argument: reflective-practice language as professional-development discourse; its attractiveness through open-mindedness and professional dialogue; its use to legitimate professional development; the rhetoric's persuasive force; workplace non-acceptance; and Fanghanel's account of dissonance between programme and workplace environments.

Arday's paragraph is not one verbatim block of Kahn's prose. It is a close rephrasing that retains the unusual argumentative sequence and several formulations. The displayed “verbatim copying and paraphrasing” label is Cofnas's characterization, not an institutional finding. Kahn cites Edwards and Nicoll (2006) and Fanghanel (2004); those underlying works remain separate provenance targets.

The Arday thesis contains a different 2006 Khan et al. report, but text search found no entry matching this 2008 article. That distinction matters when describing the uncited-source allegation.
