# Public thesis-overlap analysis

The 24 July 2026 *Analyse That* post reports a reproducibility-oriented comparison between Jason Arday’s 2015 LJMU thesis and Paula Zwozdiak-Myers’s 2009 Brunel thesis. The post says it extracted and cleaned the two PDFs, compared sentence embeddings, used two education-doctorate controls, and published the full list of 185 matched sentence pairs plus the analysis code and a six-encoder replication bundle.

## Reported measurements

The analysis reports 9 sentence pairs at cosine similarity ≥0.999 across 11.09 million focal comparisons, 179 pairs sharing at least 12 consecutive identical words, 107 spatially independent blocks, and a longest shared run of 63 words. It estimates roughly 7,000–11,000 words showing clear derivation, concentrated in the introduction and literature review; its ≥0.80 threshold table estimates 11,045 words/8.66% of cleaned thesis text, while the author warns that the threshold is a judgment-sensitive lower-bound estimate. The appendix displays the matched sentences and citation flags; it is the source for the pair-level examples, not a formal plagiarism ruling.

The post’s own conclusion is narrower than a misconduct finding: it says the documents are not independently composed in the measured material and that chronology fixes the direction for passages originating with the 2009 thesis, but says the analysis cannot establish intent or distinguish deliberate appropriation from lost provenance, intermediate materials, or other explanations. It also discloses that the article’s content was generated by Claude Opus 5, and lists limitations including only two controls, imperfect matching, quotation-detection uncertainty, and one-source coverage.

## Direct page-level check of the screenshot example

The archive independently checked the passage shown in the widely circulated comparison image. Arday’s PDF page 17 (also printed p. 17) contains the paragraph beginning “Although, student teachers on one course may experience a common programme”; Zwozdiak-Myers’s PDF page 12 contains the corresponding passage on printed p. 1, beginning “Although student teachers on one course, in very large measure, experience a common programme.” The passages share the sentence order and an extended sequence of distinctive wording, while Arday’s version makes edits and omits or changes several phrases. The page images are preserved below.

This establishes the observable textual relationship in those pages. It does not, by itself, establish intent, authorship history, permission, or an institutional plagiarism finding.

## Primary-text and reproducibility boundary

The archive already preserves Arday’s 401-page 2015 thesis at [SRC-087](2015-ljmu-phd-thesis.md). This pass adds the 370-page Brunel thesis, the analysis page, its 185-pair appendix, the Python pipeline, and the six-encoder ZIP bundle. A simple local PDF check confirms that the Brunel thesis title page identifies Paula Zwozdiak-Myers, Brunel University, a June 2009 PhD submission; the archive does not claim that a full independent reproduction has been run.

This record is therefore useful evidence of a reproducible public textual-analysis claim and of the underlying public documents. It is not an institutional investigation, a peer-reviewed assessment, or a finding that Arday committed academic misconduct. The Cambridge investigation and any LJMU/journal processes remain separate evidence tracks.
