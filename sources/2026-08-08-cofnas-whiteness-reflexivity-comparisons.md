# SRC-424 — Cofnas comparisons involving Arday’s 2018 *Whiteness and Education* article

## Source family

- Nathan Cofnas, “DEI Fraud and Cover-Up at Cambridge,” 21 July 2026: <https://ncofnas.com/p/dei-fraud-and-cover-up-at-cambridge>
- Arday, “Dismantling power and privilege through reflexivity,” DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1080/23793406.2019.1574211>
- Durham repository metadata: <https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1286002>
- Nicola Rollock, “Unspoken rules of engagement: navigating racial microaggressions in the academic terrain,” DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1080/09518398.2010.543433> (published in the 2012 volume; the DOI predates the issue)
- Rollock candidate search lead, “The invisibility of race,” DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1080/13613324.2012.638864> — retained only as a disambiguation check, not treated as the comparator
- Payne Hiraldo, “The Role of Critical Race Theory in Higher Education,” UVM landing page: <https://scholarworks.uvm.edu/tvc/vol31/iss1/7/>

This record isolates two further comparison images embedded in Cofnas’s article. It is distinct from the thesis comparisons in SRC-209 and the Arasite page audits. Cofnas’s political framing and conclusions are not adopted as facts; the images are preserved as a lead and checked against the accessible source metadata and comparator publication records.

## Rollock comparison

Cofnas’s image labels Arday (2018), p. 151, beside Rollock (2012), p. 520, and marks three examples. The first displayed pair shares the sequence about a counter-narrative making subtle aspects of racism visible, a direct association with the tools of Whiteness, and the deployment and consequences of racial micro-aggressions. The second shares the sequence about racial microaggressions being articulated and performed through slight but persistent daily recurrences, reminding people of colour they are judged different, untrustworthy, less intelligent, or inferior to White counterparts. The third retains the cited “Giles and Hughes (2009)” opening and the progression from pressures on faculty of colour in predominantly White institutions to fatigue/stress and questioning intellectual capabilities, with Arday adding or changing surrounding wording.

The image therefore shows substantial phrase-level and structural correspondence in the displayed excerpts. The archive has not obtained the full Arday article or a downloadable Rollock PDF in this pass, but the publisher’s issue page independently confirms the likely comparator’s title, author, 2012 volume/issue, and pages 517–532, including p. 520. A public search-indexed full-text manifestation also exposes the same Rollock wording shown in the image, including the “tools of Whiteness” passage, the “racial microaggressions” passage, and the Giles/Hughes paragraph. This upgrades the comparator side from an image-only lead to a corroborated text manifestation, while the Arday side remains image-mediated. The earlier “Invisibility of race” result was a title-level candidate with incompatible pagination and is explicitly not used as the comparator.

## Hiraldo comparison

Cofnas’s second image labels Arday (2018), p. 142, beside Hiraldo (2010), pp. 53–54. The excerpt shares the broad sequence about critical race theory as an analytical framework for assessing inequity in education, its use to analyse and critique educational research and practice, CRT’s role in uncovering taken-for-granted assumptions, and its relevance to diversity/inclusion in higher education. The image’s own note says there is a reference to Hiraldo (2010) and does not characterize the excerpt as verbatim copying.

This is materially weaker than the displayed Rollock comparison: the cited source is acknowledged, and the image alone does not establish whether the passage is ordinary attributed paraphrase, insufficiently marked adaptation, or something more serious. The UVM landing route initially captured only an application shell, but its DSpace API search later recovered Hiraldo’s exact 2010 item and public seven-page PDF. [SRC-575](2026-08-08-hiraldo-2010-primary-text.md) confirms that the displayed comparator text is printed on pp. 53–54; no adjudication is inferred.
