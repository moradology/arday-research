# Dewey and Van Manen segment: Arday p. 53 / Zwozdiak-Myers pp. 29–31

The Arasite `ARDAY FINAL GRID.docx` maps Arday’s printed p. 53 to Zwozdiak-Myers’s printed pp. 29–31. This record audits only that range; it does not extend the prior page audits or infer coverage of adjacent pages.

The texts share the same Dewey reflective-action question: whether reflection is limited to thought about action or is bound within action. They then use the same reference sequence around Noffke and Brennan and Grant and Zeichner. Arday inserts Noffke and Zeichner (1987), which appears in the comparator’s curated reference context, and changes some wording and punctuation.

The next passage follows the same progression from reflection as a distinctive/special form of thought, through the cycle of professional “doing,” modification of practice through reflection, and application to student teachers. Arday changes “appears to be support” to “there is some evidence to suggest some support,” adds or changes citations, and adapts the application to peer-mentoring and pedagogical practice.

Both texts then invoke Van Manen (1977: 264) and the same list of practical teaching activities—planning, adapting materials, developing courses, arranging subject matter, teaching, and evaluating—before classifying reflection as technical and routine versus technical, contextual, dialectical, and routine. The following Tinning/Grimmett sentence retains the same reference and close sentence scaffold while switching the level of reflection described.

This is a repeated textual and structural relationship across the mapped passage, not a claim that every sentence is verbatim. Arday makes substitutions, inserts study-specific material, changes citations, and renders quotations differently. The dossier’s labels are not institutional findings.
