# New Humanist — *With Reason* interview

New Humanist published a transcript of its *With Reason* episode with Arday on 15 December 2020. The conversation concerns his book *Cool Britannia and Multi-Ethnic Britain: Uncorking the Champagne Supernova*, his South London adolescence, music, race, class, and policing.

## Childhood and adolescence evidence

In the transcript, Arday says that he was born in Clapham, grew up on a council estate in relative poverty, and experienced childhood as generally positive because of his parents. He repeats the account that he did not speak until 11 and did not learn to read and write until 18. He says his mother used music to help him understand the world, that he learned guitar, and that music helped him move away from signing alongside speech therapists (transcript lines 52–58 in the preserved HTML).

He dates himself within the interview’s subject period: five at the beginning of the 1990s and 15 at its end. He recalls spending much of childhood on picket lines and protests with his mother, describes a politically and socially aware family, and says he developed an early understanding of class and social mobility through music and family activity (lines 44–48, 69–75).

He also says his father spoke to him and his brothers at 11 about the possibility of being stopped by police, and that he was first stopped at 14 or 15. This is a first-person account of adolescent experience, not a police or court record (lines 78–84, 106–111).

## Evidence boundary

This is a dated, host-published transcript and a valuable first-person source version.
