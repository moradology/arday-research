## What the source establishes

WNYC’s page identifies Melissa Harris-Perry as host and presents an interview with Arday following his Cambridge appointment. The page includes a transcript and a direct download link. The transcript is a host-produced record and carries WNYC’s warning that transcripts may be revised; the audio is the authoritative programme record.

The interview provides a distinct first-person account of Arday’s early life and literacy. Arday says he was diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder at three, that he had never known a different way of being, and that his parents—particularly his mother—did not frame his nonverbal childhood as a disadvantage. He describes observing human interaction and absorbing information without speech. He says that when he reached the University of Michigan at 20, he still had the reading age of a 12-year-old. The surrounding programme narration says he did not begin speaking until 11 and began reading and writing at 18; those milestone statements are retained as host-reported framing alongside Arday’s own account, not as independent clinical or school records.
