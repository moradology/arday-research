# 2021 special-issue editorial audit

## Identity and version

The 21-page author-accepted manuscript is titled “Special Issue Editorial: Race and Educational Leadership: The influence of research methods and critical theorising in understanding representation, roles and ethnic disparities.” The canonical record identifies Uvanney Maylor, Lorna Roberts, Kenisha Linton, and Jason Arday as authors/guest editors of the 2021 *Educational Management Administration & Leadership* special issue, DOI `10.1177/17411432211022770`. The manuscript contains anonymized “Author XXXX” placeholders in citations and does not reproduce the publisher’s final author/affiliation page; the published-version metadata is therefore retained as the stronger identity source.

## Page-level content map

- **PDF pp. 2–5 — problem framing.** The editorial sets out UK and international disparities in educational leadership and links representation to race, institutional power, and leadership context. The numerical percentages are cited literature/statistical sources discussed by the editorial, not a new dataset collected by these authors.
- **PDF pp. 6–8 — epistemology and methods.** The authors position critical race theory as a social-justice and transformative framework, describe storytelling/counter-storytelling as a way to surface marginalized experience, and discuss the limits and possible uses of quantitative research. The editorial stresses that categories and numbers require critical interrogation while allowing that quantitative work can contribute to social-justice research.
- **PDF pp. 8–9 — aims.** The stated aims cover ethnic representation/disparities, research methods and theoretical frameworks, the contexts and practices of racialized leaders, effects of race/ethnicity on leadership, and institutionalized power/inequity plus possibilities for transformation.
- **PDF pp. 9–13 — curated issue map.** The editorial summarizes six featured articles: two on school leadership and four on higher education; three of the higher-education articles focus on Black women’s leadership. It identifies the featured approaches as including critical-ecological, phenomenological, longitudinal life-history, documentary/content analysis, semi-structured interview, and critical-race-theory approaches.
- **PDF pp. 13–21 — references.** The manuscript ends with references and does not present an original participant sample, original interviews, a new coded dataset, or a standalone intervention evaluation.

## Output boundary

This is an editorial synthesis and special-issue framing paper, not an empirical Arday-only study. Its claims about disparities and about the six featured studies must be read as editorial summaries tied to the cited sources. The accepted manuscript’s anonymized placeholders and the publisher’s version-of-record access boundary are preserved rather than silently normalized.
