# *Aiming Higher*: page-level audit

## Object and authorship

The preserved 50-page mirror PDF identifies *Aiming Higher: Race, Inequality and Diversity in the Academy*, ISBN `978-1-909546-05-9`, as published by Runnymede in February 2015 and edited by Claire Alexander and Jason Arday. The first-party Runnymede PDF route returned HTTP 404; the public institutional mirror is retained as a manifestation, not relabelled as the original host file.

The contents place the introduction “Race and Higher Education” by Alexander and Arday at printed p. 4 and Arday’s chapter “Creating Space and Providing Opportunities for BME Academics in Higher Education” at printed p. 40. Other chapters are by separately named authors and are not attributed to Arday merely because he edited the volume.

## Page map

- **Printed pp. 2–5:** publication/disclaimer, foreword by David Lammy MP, and the co-authored introduction. The introduction says the collection grew from 2013–14 Runnymede Academic and Emerging Scholars Forum seminars and addresses institutional cultures, widening participation, student experience, and staffing.
- **Printed pp. 6–39:** chapters by other contributors on institutional cultures, admissions, widening participation, student experience, and staffing.
- **Printed pp. 40–41:** Arday’s chapter and its conclusion/recommendations.
- **Printed pp. 42–44:** the next contributor’s chapter and its conclusion/references.
- **Printed pp. 45–50:** contributor biographies and closing report material in the mirror manifestation.

## Arday chapter: method and content

The chapter says a February 2012 research project explored the experiences of BME academics at six universities—three in the North-West of England and three in London—and drew on the views of 12 BME academics. It says the participants held roles including Lecturer, Senior Lecturer, Reader, Professor, Head of Department, and Research Fellow and came from Asian, Black, mixed-heritage, and other ethnic backgrounds (printed p. 40).

The chapter presents anonymised participant quotations about recruitment, progression, institutional value, informal support for BME students, shortlisting, promotion, and mentoring/communities of practice. Its conclusions recommend fairer or quota-based recruitment access, professional development supporting progression, and institutional forums, learning communities, and mentorship for BME academics (printed pp. 40–41).

The PDF does not disclose recruitment procedures, response rate, interview protocol, ethics approval, coding method, transcripts, or participant-level data. The chapter is therefore a bounded qualitative account of 12 academics, not a representative survey of BME academics or an independently evaluated intervention.

## Interpretation and version boundary

This artifact supports Arday’s dated co-editorial role, his co-authored introduction, and a distinct authored chapter with a disclosed six-university/12-participant project. It does not establish that he authored the other chapters, independently verified every statistic cited in the volume, or that its recommendations were implemented. Preserve the 404 first-party route and mirror PDF as separate provenance states; do not create a second publication node.
