## What is established

The Ruling Passions project identifies Arday as the guest for episode 2 and describes a conversation about music, race, autistic masking, vulnerability, motivation, academia and autistic experience. The page includes a timestamped transcript and embeds the Spotify recording. The transcript is especially valuable for the biography because it preserves first-person statements: at approximately 2:41–5:30 he discusses his autism diagnosis at 3, speaking at 11, learning to read and write at 18, and completing a PhD within 12 years; around 41:45–42:30 he recalls his mother reading to him and playing Enya during childhood. These remain self-reported interview statements, not independent clinical or family records.

Additional first-person passages are useful for understanding Arday’s self-conception and working method. Around 11:08–11:53 he says that motivation became harder after receiving a professorship, describes himself as not trained as a sociologist but self-trained, and uses a long-standing tailoring obsession beginning around age 14 to distinguish intrinsic interest from the more externally driven work of race research. Around 14:03–15:33 he describes masking autistic behaviours in public as increasingly difficult and exhausting, and distinguishes it from racial or class code-switching. Around 46:58–50:14 he says his paper titles deliberately draw on song titles, describes music as a central autistic lens for making sense of the world, and argues for more accessible, “melodic” writing outside the rigid incentives of peer review. These are dated interview testimony and public intellectual positions, not clinical findings or proof of the quality of his scholarship.

The Sheffield Hallam University Research Archive separately preserves the project’s published *Ruling Passions: A Research Informed Handbook* (2023), made for the ESRC Festival of Social Sciences. Its printed p. 56 podcast guide identifies Episode 2 as “Jason Arday” and summarizes its topics as race, autistic masking, vulnerability, intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, expectations of academia, and the importance of music. This is project-level corroboration of the episode identity and topic framing, not a transcript or independent verification of the interview’s autobiographical content.
