# Page-level audit

The complete 17-page PDF was extracted with layout preservation and reviewed against rendered pages from the opening, childhood/charity passage, Sandro passage, later interview, and closing notes. The interview was conducted at Cambridge on 28 November 2023 and published in *Revista História Hoje* volume 13, issue 29. The PDF is a Portuguese translation and editorial adaptation by Amilcar Araujo Pereira, not a verbatim English transcript.

This is a new audit record, not a new interview node. The DOI family remains canonical under [SRC-480](2024-12-11-rhhj-jason-arday-interview.md) and the earlier capture record [SRC-216](2024-rhhj-jason-arday-interview.md); the preserved PDF and text derivatives are byte-identical across the local paths.

## Page map and evidence boundaries

- **Opening biography — PDF pp. 1–2 / printed pp. 249–250:** the interviewer’s introduction repeats the familiar diagnosis, speech/literacy, education, Cambridge, and charity narrative. These are interviewer framing and source-propagated biography, not first-person answers or independent records.
- **Institutional power and antiracism — PDF pp. 2–4 / printed pp. 250–252:** in response to the Cambridge question, Arday discusses endowment, colonial inheritance, redistribution, conformity pressure, identity, and the desire for antiracism to become ordinary institutional practice. These passages document his public position, not an audit of Cambridge’s finances or institutional conduct.
- **Family and childhood — PDF pp. 4–6 / printed pp. 252–254:** Arday gives first-person accounts of Ghanaian and South African parents, family poverty, parents feeding people with little money, childhood shelter visits, unilateral deafness, and the Christmas shelter encounter at 18. Existing ledger claims retain these as autobiographical testimony; the interview supplies no civil, medical, shelter, or household records.
- **Charity and formative encounter — PDF pp. 6–7 / printed pp. 254–255:** he links the shelter encounter to a charity mission, reports a £1-million-before-40 goal, says he reached it at 30, and reports £5 million raised by the interview date. These are self-reported aggregate figures and remain separate from fundraiser-level records and later institutional totals.
- **Sandro and intellectual formation — PDF pp. 7–8 and 13 / printed pp. 255–256 and 262:** he repeats Sandro’s “will versus ability” lesson and later names Paulo Freire, bell hooks, Shirley Anne Tate, Patricia Hill Collins, Kimberlé Crenshaw, Steve Biko, and Nelson Mandela as formative influences. This is testimony about influence and identity, not an independently reconstructed intellectual genealogy.
- **Praxis and community knowledge — PDF pp. 8–12 / printed pp. 257–261:** Arday argues that universities need stronger relationships with community activists and elders, that academics should bring practical knowledge into the university, and that they should work with rather than extract from community activists. He also describes strategic calm when confronting racism, using the metaphor “body in flames, head in the fridge.” These are attributed political/pedagogical positions, not measured institutional findings.
- **Curriculum and educational purpose — PDF pp. 12–16 / printed pp. 261–264:** he identifies Paulo Freire and Black feminist thinkers as pedagogical influences, argues for broader concepts of excellence and safer university spaces, rejects simple meritocratic explanations of racial inequality, and describes education as preparation for a multicultural society. The interview records his argument, not an evaluation of curriculum or employment outcomes.
- **Closing — PDF p. 17 / printed p. 265:** Arday says his next dream is to teach in Brazil. The notes confirm the interview date, translation/editorial process, and the interviewer’s then-current British Academy Visiting Professor role. These are source-version details.

## Interpretation boundary

The strongest evidence here is that Arday publicly gave these autobiographical accounts and positions in a dated, identifiable interview. The article does not independently verify the childhood, medical, family, shelter, charity, employment, or institutional claims embedded in the conversation. Translation and editing mean that English wording should not be reconstructed as a verbatim quotation without an English source or the original recording.
