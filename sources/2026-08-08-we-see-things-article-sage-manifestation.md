# *We’ll see things they’ll never see*: SAGE manifestation

SAGE’s indexed PDF manifestation exposes the complete 23-page article text in the browser reader. It identifies Chantelle Jessica Lewis and Jason Arday as authors of *The Sociological Review* 71(6), 2023, pp. 1299–1321, DOI `10.1177/00380261231184357`. The article describes itself as a collaborative autoethnography/dialogical knowledge-production project about the lived experiences of two Black neurodivergent sociologists in UK higher education.

## Bounded article facts

- The abstract says the authors use dialogical knowledge production and collaborative autoethnography to discuss race, neurodiversity, and “neurotypical hegemony” in UK higher education (PDF p. 1).
- The introduction describes the article as a retrospective reflective discussion based on the experiences of two Black neurodivergent sociologists employed at different UK universities (PDF pp. 2–3).
- The article records no financial support for the research, authorship, or publication (PDF p. 19 / printed p. 1318).
- A note says the title is a lyric from Oasis’s “Live Forever,” chosen to pay homage to Arday’s mother, Gifty Arday, who used music to help him understand social interactions (PDF p. 19 / printed p. 1318). This is authorial article text, not independent family corroboration.
- A separate note says the authors use the term “Asperger’s syndrome” for Arday’s diagnosis while acknowledging contemporary objections to that eponym (PDF p. 19 / printed p. 1318). This is a self-authored publication statement, not a clinical record.
- The references cite Mobeen, Mugaju, and Arday’s March 2023 BBC *Outlook* broadcast “At 18, I could not read, now I’m a Cambridge professor,” BBC Sounds ID `w3ct4r2y`; that is logged as a separate media-retrieval lead.

## Evidence boundary

The article is intentionally autobiographical/autoethnographic and should be used as a source for the authors’ stated experiences, concepts, and method. Its self-reported diagnosis and family/music narrative must remain distinct from clinical, family, or childhood records. Indexed article text establishes publication content and page references; it does not resolve questions about the truth of autobiographical claims or research-integrity allegations.
