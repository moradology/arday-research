# Methods-passage textual correspondence

This is a distinct sentence-level follow-up to [SRC-383](2026-08-07-methods-lineage-audit.md). It compares the preserved primary PDFs rather than relying on the earlier broad observation that the papers use similar qualitative procedures.

## Compared passages

Memon (2016), PDF p. 2, says that each participant completed a “self-administered demographic questionnaire”; that discussions were facilitated by a researcher “trained and experienced in cross cultural working and qualitative methods”; that each session was “audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim”; and that written notes were taken “using flipcharts” so participants’ views were clearly documented.

Arday (2018), PDF p. 10, says that participants completed an “anonymous self-administered questionnaire”; that discussions were facilitated by a researcher with experience in “cross-cultural working and qualitative methods”; that all sessions and interviews were “audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim”; and that “written notes were also taken and flipcharts were utilized” so participants’ views were clearly documented. It then says that participants were encouraged to express their own views and that conversations enriched the discussion, a sequence also present in Memon’s immediately following methods text.

The correspondence therefore includes a distinctive sequence of questionnaire, cross-cultural/qualitative-methods facilitator description, audio recording, verbatim transcription, written notes, flipcharts, documented participant views, and participant expression. It is stronger than a generic “both papers used interviews” observation. The papers nevertheless differ in sample, setting, dates, questionnaire purpose, focus-group duration, participant numbers, and other methodological details.

## Attribution and inference boundary

The Arday article cites Memon in the surrounding literature and later states that its two broad themes were “adapted from the work of Memon et al. (2016).” The methods passage itself does not contain a Memon citation in the compared location. The textual correspondence is directly observable, but this record does not determine whether it resulted from copying, shared drafting, common source material, conventional method description, deficient attribution, or another explanation. It does not establish intent, authorship, permission, plagiarism, fraud, or an institutional finding. The 2023 correction is a separate editorial record and should not be treated as adjudication of this passage.
