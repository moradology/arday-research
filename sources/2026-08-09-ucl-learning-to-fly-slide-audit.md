## Slide-level extraction

- **Slides 1–3 (C-1139):** The deck identifies the lecture as “Learning to Fly: Engaging pedagogically with race and racism in Higher Education,” delivered by Arday at UCL on 16 March 2020. Its declared themes are decolonising the curriculum, intersectionality, inclusive pedagogies, unconscious bias, and centring race and racism in curricula. This establishes presentation scope and authorship, not the truth of the propositions that follow.
- **Slides 4–5 (C-1140):** The deck frames Whiteness as an instrument of power and privilege, argues that dominant White European canons omit particular knowledge, and links curriculum omission to engagement, inclusivity, belonging, stereotypes, and systemic reproduction of racism for BAME learners. The slides cite Peters (2015) and Arday (2020); they are an authored argument with citations, not an independent review of those sources.
- **Slides 6–10 (C-1141):** Proposed or discussed interventions include curriculum design, reading lists, Black and ethnic-minority student engagement, library engagement, intersectionality, seeing oneself reflected in university communities, and diversifying knowledge gatekeepers. The deck presents these as pedagogical/institutional considerations rather than documenting implementation.
- **Slides 11–12 (C-1142):** The deck presents competing or qualified framings of unconscious-bias training: one slide calls it a comfortable institutional explanation for racial disparities, while the next describes rapid judgments and training approaches before noting that deeply ingrained biases may resist change. Both slides cite Tate and Page (2018), so the wording must not be silently treated as Arday’s original empirical finding.
- **Slides 13–15 (C-1143):** The deck calls for open conversations about race and racism, reduced hierarchy and reciprocal learning, diversified teaching staff, collaborative BME student voice in curriculum design, recognition of burdens on BME staff and students (including women), and collective responsibility for dismantling racism. Slide 15 presents UCL’s Inclusive Curriculum Health Check and its reported attainment-gap rationale as an institutional example; the deck does not independently evaluate the project.
