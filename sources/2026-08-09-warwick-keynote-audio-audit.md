## Approximate subject windows

- **00:00–08:10 — pedagogy, freedom, constraint, and hope.** Arday frames the classroom as a space of freedom and artistry, then discusses institutional, performance, and consumerist pressures that can narrow agency and the human purpose of education.
- **08:10–12:00 — vulnerability and the generous assumption.** He advocates two-way knowledge construction, says he deliberately does not use slides, and argues for presuming that learners know rather than applying deficit assumptions, especially to minoritized groups.
- **12:00–15:30 — teaching failure and fear.** He recounts a poor teaching session, a child’s question about whether he had “hit free,” and his fear of getting into trouble. This is a first-person anecdote, not an independently verified childhood or employment record.
- **15:30–21:00 — complaint anxiety and collective empowerment.** He discusses fear of complaint, compliance, failure, safe spaces, and educators bringing people with them. These are his public pedagogical positions.
- **22:30–30:55 — power, leadership, and institutional jeopardy.** He argues that senior leaders can distribute power in ways that remove agency and describes people receiving warnings, gross-misconduct consequences, or losing work after classroom remarks concerning racism, sexism, homophobia, ableism, or religious inequality. This preserves his account as a reported anecdote/public position; it is not an independently verified institutional finding.
- **31:49–35:57 — humility and safe failure.** In response to a question about safe failure, he advocates humility, vulnerability, kindness, dignity, belonging, reducing hierarchy, modelling failure, and measuring success partly by whether one brought others along.
- **36:53–41:07 — misrecognition and deficit assumptions.** He recounts being mistaken for a student or domestic staff and links that experience to his argument for presuming people’s knowledge and whole personhood. This is first-person testimony plus public position; exact wording awaits human listening.
- **42:24–45:18 — intelligence, luck, and imposter syndrome.** He discusses intelligence as a colonial inheritance, distinguishes wisdom, skill, and will, says he does not experience imposter syndrome, and acknowledges luck and unequal rewards. These are self-report and public position, not psychological or causal findings.
