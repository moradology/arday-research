# *The Broken Pipeline*: page-level audit

## Object and authorship

The preserved eight-page PDF is titled *The Broken Pipeline: Barriers to Black PhD Students Accessing Research Council Funding*. Its cover dates it September 2019 and names Paulette Williams, Sukhi Bath, Dr Jason Arday, and Chantelle Lewis. It is a Leading Routes policy/roundtable report, not a peer-reviewed article and not a sole-authored Arday publication.

## Page map and method

- **PDF p. 1:** title, four named authors, September 2019 date, and Leading Routes branding.
- **PDF p. 2:** Leading Routes’ mission, aims, activities, prior focus groups, events, and supporters. These are organisational self-description and activity claims.
- **PDF pp. 3–4:** the February 2019 closed roundtable, its purpose, discussion questions, contextual statistics, and background discussion of attainment and postgraduate research inequalities.
- **PDF p. 5:** barriers raised in the discussion and recommendations for data, admissions, assessment, panels, research experience, funding, and supervision.
- **PDF p. 6:** Leading Routes’ planned 2019–20 responses, including events, Wellcome internships, further research, consultancy, media, network, and festival activity.
- **PDF pp. 7–8:** bibliography and contact information.

The report says the roundtable brought together sector professionals, students, academics, higher-education institutions, and research-funding representatives. It does not publish a participant roster, number of attendees, transcript, recruitment procedure, coding method, or survey instrument. Its observations are a structured discussion synthesis, not a population estimate or a transparent qualitative dataset.

## Statistics and interpretation boundary

The report cites HESA and a UKRI Freedom of Information response for contextual figures. It gives 15,560 full-time UK-domiciled first-year PhD students in 2017/18, of whom 3% were Black, and 245 Black or Black Mixed awards among 19,868 UKRI-funded studentships across 2016/17–2018/19, including 30 Black Caribbean students (PDF p. 3). These figures are attributed external statistics; the report does not present the underlying administrative data or an independent reanalysis.

The report’s discussion identifies prior-attainment filters, research-intensive-institution preferences, limited applicant/conversion data, scholarship-eligibility concerns, variable guidance, fixed notions of academic excellence, and potential supervisory bias/microaggressions. Its recommendations include better applicant-level data, broader assessment, diverse panels, transparent admissions, paid research experience, possible ring-fenced funding, and team supervision (PDF p. 5). These are roundtable outputs and policy proposals, not tested causal findings.

## Follow-up claims

The final substantive page lists Leading Routes’ planned 2019–20 activities, including four “Staying the Course” events, two paid Wellcome summer-internship places, further research, consultancy, a YouTube/podcast series, network support, and a Black in Academia festival. The PDF records commitments as stated at publication; it does not establish delivery, participant numbers, or impact.

The report therefore supports a dated co-authorship edge and a source-bounded account of Arday’s policy work. It should not be used as independent confirmation of the cited statistics, as evidence of a representative sample, or as proof that its recommendations were implemented.
