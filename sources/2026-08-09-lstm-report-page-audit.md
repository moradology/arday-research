# Scope

This audit reads the substantive pages of LSTM’s 21-page *Equity & Inclusion at LSTM: Improving racial equity in global medicine* report and extracts non-duplicate propositions about its method, stated findings, recommendations, and implementation logic. The report identifies Professor Jason Arday as author and independent consultant (SRC-536/C-681); this record does not re-count that authorship claim.

## Page map

- **pp. 10–11 — consultation logic:** the framework is described as structuring the review and action plan while leaving room for themes to emerge from local context. The report says qualitative consultation centres the lived experiences of staff, students, and stakeholders of colour.
- **p. 12 — method boundary:** the report records a disparity between desk-review material and participant experience, calls this a “principle implementation gap,” and says the mixed method combined document review with staff/student voices. It records three participation indicators: 45 individual staff interviews, 10 staff focus groups with 72 participants, and one student focus group with 9 participants. It says direct quotations were omitted for sensitivity and confidentiality.
- **pp. 13–19 — themes and recommendations:** the report presents participant-experience sections on culture; recruitment, progression and promotion; student experience; leadership and communication; academic and professional services; and reporting processes. Recommendations include senior-leadership accountability, representation, curriculum decolonisation, career-pipeline and mentoring interventions, student development, intersectional monitoring, review of HR criteria, support and consequences around racism reports, lessons-learned processes, restorative approaches, and cultural-competence training.
- **pp. 20–21 — synthesis and implementation:** the conclusion foregrounds leadership, culture, exclusion, global-partnership opportunities, progression, reporting/management of racism, and intersectionality, particularly the experiences of Black women and other women of colour. It says these findings informed standards and indicators in an LSTM-specific framework, and the final page says Arday would work with LSTM for 14–16 months on implementation.

## Evidence boundary

These are claims made by a commissioned report about its consultation and analysis. They are not independently re-audited participant testimony, a clinical or employment record, or proof that the recommendations were implemented or effective. The report’s dated author biography remains report copy and is not upgraded by this audit.
