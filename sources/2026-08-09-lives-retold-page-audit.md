# Lives Retold / BBC Lent Talk — page-level audit

The 11-page PDF recovered from a 28 June 2023 Wayback capture identifies itself as a transcript of Jason Arday’s BBC Radio 4 Lent Talk broadcast on 20 March 2021. The first five chapters are presented as a transcript; the final “Books” chapter is publisher-style description. This audit records the page-bounded autobiographical material and the source-version conflicts it creates. It does not treat the transcript as independent verification.

## Page-level contents

- **PDF p. 2:** Arday writes that he was born deaf in one ear, had “global developmental delay,” signed until 11, and learned to read and write at 18. He describes music as central to constructing ideas about the world and names specific songs.
- **PDF p. 4:** He gives his first words at 11 as “Hello, my name is Jason,” after speech therapy. He says he had been a youth player at a professional football club and was on the verge of turning professional at snooker. The same page places his first stop-and-search at 14 and contains autobiographical accounts of violence and loss in his neighbourhood.
- **PDF p. 6:** He says that, after learning to read and write, he attended university two years later; completed a first Master’s at 25; completed 30 marathons in 35 days at 25; completed a second Master’s and a 300-mile treadmill challenge at 26; ran with the Olympic torch and completed 500 miles from Edinburgh to London in six days at 27; earned his PhD by 30; and reached £1 million raised for charity. These are self-reported chronology and achievement claims, and the endurance wording conflicts with later 600-mile/six-day and 600-mile/12-day versions.
- **PDF p. 6:** In the same autobiographical passage he writes: “After completing my PhD I was diagnosed with two brain tumours which thankfully turned out to be benign.” This is materially different from the 2021 Sanger interview, where he says a brain tumour was removed two weeks before his viva and that he had a stroke immediately afterwards. The archive preserves both statements as source versions; it does not infer whether they refer to separate episodes, a chronology error, different medical descriptions, or a transcription/retelling problem.
- **PDF pp. 7–8:** He narrates an April 2019 interview for an academic position, says he received a call a day or two later, and identifies the job as at Durham University. He frames this as the culmination of his professional ambition and says he was then an Associate Professor and Deputy Executive Dean.

## Evidence boundary

This is a dated, archived, first-person transcript and therefore strong evidence of what this public broadcast artifact says. It is not a medical record, school record, race log, charity audit, employment file, or independent witness. The PDF’s source note says the first five chapters are a transcript of the BBC talk; the “Books” chapter is publisher copy and should not be treated as additional autobiographical testimony.
