# Page audit

This audit reads the 22-page Glasgow “Published Version” of Arday’s *British Journal of Sociology of Education* article at printed-page level. It expands canonical record [SRC-051](2022-more-to-prove-more-to-lose.md) without creating a duplicate publication. The article is a qualitative study of precarious employment among staff of colour; the reported findings are bounded by its own sampling and limitations.

## Design and methods

- **Research frame — printed pp. 513–518:** the article uses critical race theory (CRT) and thematic analysis to examine race, racism, job security, career progression, and mental health in higher-education precarity. The CRT discussion explicitly foregrounds race/racism, intersectionality, social construction, whiteness as property, challenges to neutrality/meritocracy, and experiential knowledge.
- **Participants — printed p. 519:** convenience sampling through networks and recommendations recruited 18 staff of colour across 10 UK universities, aged 27–52. The article reports 13 academic staff and five professional-services staff; participants identified as Black/Black British, Mixed Heritage, Asian/Asian British, and Latin-American. Most were on hourly or fixed-term contracts.
- **Analysis and positionality — printed pp. 519–520:** the article says thematic analysis used deductive inquiry informed by CRT, with latent themes, an evolving coding frame, and iterative adaptation of themes/sub-themes. It also discusses researcher positionality and rejects treating reflexivity as if subjectivity could simply be bracketed away; this is an explicit epistemological/methodological position, not a claim that the researcher’s perspective is absent.
- **Procedure — printed p. 520:** participants completed anonymous self-administered questionnaires whose monitoring information and comments informed focus-group and interview schedules. They then took part in semi-structured interviews and unstructured focus groups. The article says two focus groups lasted roughly two hours, consent was obtained, sessions were audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim, and written notes/flipcharts documented participant views.

## Findings, limits, and recommendations

- **Reported themes — printed pp. 521–527:** the article identifies systemic racism, job insecurity, and lack of career progression. Subthemes include exploitation, dehumanisation, gendered racism, financial insecurity, work–life balance, poor mental health, transience, and progression barriers. These are the article’s thematic interpretation of the 18-person qualitative study, not population estimates.
- **Limitations — printed p. 528:** the article expressly says the participant group is specific and the findings are not generalisable. It presents the study as illuminating discriminatory patterns rather than measuring their prevalence, and says broader perspectives and quantitative work would be needed to examine causality or interaction/mediation.
- **Implications — printed pp. 528–530:** the article treats precarious employment and racism as public-health concerns, calls for clearer definitions and more standardised rights/benefits across contract types, and recommends action by universities, legislators, policymakers, and funders. It argues that implementation should address racialised and gendered experience rather than treating “staff of colour” as homogeneous.

## Provenance and version boundary

The preserved PDF is the Glasgow repository’s “Published Version,” with DOI `10.1080/01425692.2022.2074375`, online publication date 25 June 2022, and pages 513–533. Durham’s 2020 *Living Hand to Mouth* record remains a separate submitted-manuscript lead: its full text is not locally available, so this audit does not collapse or compare the two versions. The methods sequence also resembles procedures recorded in other Arday/Memon sources, but this audit does not make a copying or misconduct finding; a distinct sentence-level comparison would require a separately scoped comparator record.
