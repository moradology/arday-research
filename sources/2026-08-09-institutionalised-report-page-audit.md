# *Institutionalised*: page-level audit

## Object and authorship

The preserved PDF is a 60-page January 2021 report titled *Institutionalised: The Rise of Islamophobia in Higher Education*. The cover and front matter identify Sofia Akel, Race Equity Lead at London Metropolitan University’s Centre for Equity and Inclusion, as the report’s producer/author. The contents page places three forewords on printed pages 5–7, followed by the executive summary, background, methodology, findings, recommendations, and bibliography.

Jason Arday’s contribution is the foreword on printed PDF p. 7, titled “A New Dawn: Addressing Islamophobia in British Higher Education.” The report labels him there as “Dr Jason Arday FRSA,” Visiting Professor at the Centre for Equity & Inclusion, Associate Professor in Sociology and Deputy Executive Dean (People and Culture) at Durham, and Runnymede Trust trustee. These are dated report copy, not current-status records.

## Arday’s foreword

The foreword (PDF p. 7) argues that Islamophobia is a form of institutional racism and that higher education can harbour and facilitate it. It describes public and policy narratives that racialise Muslims as associated with terrorism or civilisational threat, and calls for universities to implement interventions that disrupt Islamophobia and advance racial equality. The foreword endorses the London Metropolitan report as a contribution to anti-racist work and describes its institutional definition of Islamophobia as a challenge to the sector.

This is direct evidence of Arday’s dated public position and of his credited foreword role. It is not evidence that he authored the report’s research questions, surveys, analysis, findings, or recommendations.

## Report method and evidence boundary

The report’s methodology (PDF pp. 29–32; printed pp. 29–32) asks how institutional Islamophobia shapes Muslim students’ and staff’s experiences at London Metropolitan University. It reports two separate closed/open-ended surveys: 93 student respondents and 13 staff respondents. It says face-to-face interviews and focus groups were not possible during the COVID-19 pandemic and that online versions were not conducted because of sensitivity and privacy concerns; the report calls the absence of qualitative research a weakness.

Data collection is described as taking place in November 2020 through internal university, students’ union, communications, and staff channels. The report says the survey was not publicly distributed, describes anonymity/confidentiality precautions, and notes five responses from non-Muslim people despite the intended Muslim-only target. The report presents descriptive breakdowns by ethnicity, gender, and student study level, while withholding granular staff detail to protect anonymity.

The findings and recommendations (PDF pp. 33–54; printed pp. 33–54) belong to the London Metropolitan research report. They should not be back-attributed to Arday merely because he contributed the foreword. The PDF is a single-institution survey report with a disclosed response count and method limitation, not a national prevalence study or an Arday-authored empirical paper.

## Source-version and ontology boundary

The PDF establishes a 2021 report, a named producer, a distinct Arday foreword, and the foreword’s dated institutional labels and public arguments. It does not independently corroborate those role labels, and it does not establish that Arday conducted or supervised the underlying research. Keep the report, foreword, author, institution, method, and public-position edges separate in the ontology. Reopen for a corrected report, underlying survey instrument/data, ethics documentation, implementation evidence, or an independent record of Arday’s role.
