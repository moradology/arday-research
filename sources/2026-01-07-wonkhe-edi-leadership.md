# “The fragile future of EDI demands bold university leadership” — Wonkhe

Wonkhe republishes the 7 January 2026 commentary attributed to Arun Verma and Jason Arday. The Wonkhe manifestation exposes the substantive article body that was absent from the original Quality Strategy Network HTML capture. It is a separate host/version of the same dated commentary, not a second publication.

## Content record

The article argues that higher-education institutions should defend inclusion, equity, and academic freedom publicly. It presents universities as institutions with a civic role and describes them as a “critical mirror” for social, cultural, and political narratives. The authors argue that risk aversion can make institutional EDI work performative, and that renaming or absorbing EDI into safer labels such as organisational development or social responsibility can obscure the work’s purpose.

The “Another American import” section frames U.S. political pressure on EDI spending as a threat to university autonomy. It invokes McKinsey diversity/leadership correlations, a University of Oxford/UCL Policy Lab/More in Common finding about public attitudes to EDI, and statements from UCL and Harvard as supporting context. Those are the article’s cited/linked argumentative sources; this archive does not treat the commentary as an independent audit of those underlying studies or institutional statements.

The conclusion calls on institutions to reaffirm commitments to EDI, equitable access, educational freedom, and liberty. These are authored policy positions and normative argument, not evidence that any institution adopted or implemented the proposed commitments.
