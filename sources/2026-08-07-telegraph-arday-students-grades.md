## Record

The archived article reports that former Cambridge students who contacted *The Telegraph* complained of “inadequate” supervision by Arday, including claims that some were told they did not need supervision because they were outstanding and later received lower-than-expected grades. It reports one student’s account that a request to change supervisor was met with an accusation of racism, while expressly noting that the article does not suggest Arday made that accusation.

The report says former students were considering appeals to Cambridge, including at least one student whose earlier appeal had been rejected, and that graduates were considering legal action. It also reports demands for tuition-fee refunds and identifies Daniel Sokol’s attributed explanation of possible university-appeal, ombudsman, and litigation routes. These are reported complaints and legal/administrative possibilities, not findings that grades were wrong, supervision was legally deficient, or Arday committed misconduct toward students.

The article’s chronology also reports Cambridge’s senior-hiring review and describes reported prior handling of allegations, including the allocation of a “responsible person” and a separate investigation request. Those process claims are retained as *Telegraph* reporting and cross-linked to the Cambridge institutional records; they do not replace the underlying case materials.
