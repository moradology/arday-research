## Reproducible passages

- **24:30–26:30:** Arday says he grew up in poverty in a family of four boys and describes his father’s account of a “beauty” in being poor. He says his mother was highly educated, took substantial time out to care for him and his brother, and that both were autistic. He describes global developmental delay and says he could not speak until 11.
- **26:30–29:30:** He says he signed until 11, attended a special-needs school in Wandsworth that he says has since closed, and identifies Clapham, South London, as his home area. He recalls being 18 and unable to count money, read, or write, and identifies Sandro as his college tutor. He also describes his mother placing him in difficult social situations and a study involving educational psychologists that he says ran from age 3 to 25.
- **29:30–32:30:** He connects music and his mother reading to him with his development, names Enya as a recurring childhood listening memory, describes speech therapy from roughly 3 to 16, and credits Sandro with changing his life. He says his wife, whom he met at 18, helped teach him to read and write using phonics materials while training as a primary teacher.
