# Protocol page audit

The preserved BMJ Open article is a nine-page version-of-record PDF in the Oxford Research Archive manifestation. This audit binds the existing publication record to the protocol’s stated design and to the contribution statement that specifically names Jason Arday. It does not treat a protocol as evidence that recruitment, analysis, or dissemination was completed.

## What the protocol says

- **Scope and design — PDF p. 1:** the study is designed for under-represented young people aged 16–24 in England, including a separate focus on autistic young people. It proposes narrative inquiry, thematic and intersectional analysis, and meaningful involvement of young people in shaping the research, coding data, and interpreting findings.
- **Research architecture — PDF pp. 2–4:** Work Package 1 proposes purposeful sampling of up to 100 young people across community, NHS-waitlist, and online routes; Work Package 2 proposes 30 autistic young people recruited through the Cornwall CIDER centre and related consented routes. The tables describe intersectionality as both a conceptual lens and a way to attend to multiple disadvantage, place, identity, and structural hierarchy.
- **Participation and adjustments — PDF pp. 4–6:** youth peer researchers, a Research Advisory Group of up to 12 young people, and youth codesigners are built into the proposed research process. The autism work package proposes alternative formats, autistic representation at multiple levels, and adjustments intended to support meaningful participation.
- **Data and ethics — PDF pp. 5–8:** the protocol describes purposeful sampling, consent at multiple stages, audio recordings and transcripts, password-protected study-team storage, HRA and Health and Care Research Wales approval (IRAS 340259; REC 24/SC/0083), a distress protocol, and regular ethical review involving the advisory and steering groups.
- **Arday’s named contribution — PDF p. 8:** the contributor statement says “JA leads work package 1 with KB and substantially contributed to the writing of the application.” It also says all listed authors contributed substantially to writing the protocol and approved the submitted version. This is direct authorship/contribution evidence from the publication, not an inference from author order or affiliation.
- **Funding and affiliation topology — PDF pp. 7–8:** the protocol gives NIHR Programme Grants for Applied Research award NIHR204148 as funding and identifies the University of Oxford as sponsor. Arday’s superscript `3` maps to the Faculty of Education, Cambridge University; the Oxford sponsorship and repository location do not establish Oxford employment for him.

## Evidence boundary

This record strengthens the subject’s scholarly-output and research-role map: it documents a named lead role in WP1 and substantial protocol-writing contribution. It does not establish that Arday was principal investigator, that the planned sample was recruited, that the protocol’s proposed methods were followed, or that later results support its hypotheses. It is also not evidence about Arday’s personal diagnosis or about the study population beyond the protocol’s inclusion criteria.
