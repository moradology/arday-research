## What the snapshots say

The site title is “30 in 35 – Jason Arday Marathon Man,” and its description repeats “30 marathons in 35 days.” The 18 August Q&A is written in first person and says Arday and friends chose the challenge after he had set himself a goal at age 17 of raising £100,000 before age 25. It says his day job was Sports Lecturer at Richmond upon Thames College in Twickenham, and describes seven months of training: 12–14 miles per day, including a 4 a.m.–5:30 a.m. six-mile run and a further eight miles after work.

The Q&A says the marathons generally began at 3 a.m. to avoid pedestrians and heat, and names several routes: North Greenwich–Teddington / Teddington–Putney, Piccadilly Circus–Teddington / return, and Tower Bridge–Teddington / return to Clapham Junction. It also names Shooting Stars Children’s Hospice and Shelter as the beneficiary charities, identifies his daughter Taylah as a personal source of motivation, and describes recovery methods including ice baths, massage, warm baths, water aerobics, sleep, and compression tights.

The page-4 snapshot records posts dated 9–10 August 2010. The “Marathon No. 21” post says “9 to go”; “Marathon No. 22” gives a time of 5 hours 19 minutes and says “22 down, 8 to go.” The home snapshot contains a 17 August “Marathon No. 29” post saying “1 to go,” plus an 18 August Q&A and 20 August final-run logistics. The latter invite supporters to meet at Sloane Square tube station at 12:15 p.m. for the final two miles, finish at Piccadilly Circus at about 1 p.m., and says TV crews were expected. These posts establish the contemporaneous campaign narrative and planned finish; they do not by themselves establish an audited completion or final funds raised.

## Access boundary

The supplied HTML snapshots were captured successfully and retain the original post text, titles, dates, author metadata, embedded YouTube references, and links to campaign photographs. A bounded attempt to recover the linked image binaries from the archived `im_` endpoints returned Wayback 404/connection failures for several photographs; the image URLs are retained in `image-urls.txt` and are not represented as locally recovered images. Several directly requested secondary page URLs returned the Wayback shell rather than usable original content and are not treated as additional evidence. The campaign blog is therefore a strong contemporaneous narrative source, but not an audited charity statement, clinical record, employment record, or complete site mirror.
