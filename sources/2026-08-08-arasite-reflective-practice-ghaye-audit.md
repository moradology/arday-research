# Ghaye reflective-practice segment: Arday pp. 46–47 / Zwozdiak-Myers pp. 17–18

## Scope and materials

The Arasite `ARDAY FINAL GRID.docx` inside the preserved `plag4.zip` maps Arday’s printed pp. 46–47 to Zwozdiak-Myers’s printed pp. 17–18. This record audits only that mapped block. It does not extend SRC-416’s audit of Arday pp. 35–36 / Zwozdiak-Myers pp. 9–10, the earlier pp. 57–69 audit family, or the later pp. 75–77 and pp. 79–81 audits.

The comparison used the locally preserved LJMU and Brunel thesis PDFs, with printed pagination checked against rendered page images. The grid’s labels are dossier-author characterizations, not institutional findings.

## Direct comparison

Both texts introduce the same subsection-level discussion by describing reflective practice as a discourse in which student teachers use language and frame questions about teaching and experience, followed by the same Ghaye and Ghaye (1998: 34) typology. They then present the same five-category sequence: descriptive, perceptive, receptive, interactive, and critical reflection, with the same short descriptions and the same final “broader system” framing. Arday adds punctuation and a Ghaye (2011) citation; the comparator has minor typography differences.

The following paragraph preserves the same progression from the purpose of reflection, through the direction of reflective conversations and questions, to the finding that critical reflective conversations are less frequent than descriptive ones. Both texts then use the same Hatton and Smith, Macdonald and Brooker, and Tsangaridou reference grouping and move to the claim that student teachers vary in professional-development stage and may need guidance when positioning practice within ideological and political contexts.

Arday’s version changes the opening sentence of the paragraph, inserts “collaborative endeavour” and other study-specific wording, adds or changes citations, and uses “more directional influence or guidance” where the comparator uses “more guidance.” The relationship is therefore not described as an uninterrupted verbatim block. Its significance is the repeated sequence, the identical five-part typology, multiple close sentence frames, and shared distinctive reference cluster across the mapped pages.

## Interpretation boundary

This is a reproducible page-level textual and structural relationship in two public primary texts. It does not determine intent, authorship history, whether an intermediate source was used, the applicable quotation/citation standard, the role of supervision, or whether any institution found plagiarism or other misconduct. The audit does not convert the Arasite grid’s labels into an institutional finding and does not infer coverage of pages outside pp. 46–47 / 17–18.
