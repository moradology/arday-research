# BBC Newsnight — page audit

The BBC News article published on 17 November 2020 reports a Newsnight analysis of PhD-offer data and includes several attributed statements from Jason Arday. This audit separates the BBC’s reported dataset and article framing from Arday’s testimony. The linked broadcast, raw FOI returns, and full programme transcript were not recovered in this pass.

## Reported analysis

- BBC says it sent freedom-of-information requests to 133 UK universities for academic years 2015–2020; 62 universities responded, and all but one had a higher offer rate for white applicants.
- Of 51 universities providing a detailed ethnic breakdown, Black applicants had the lowest successful-offer proportion at 33 institutions.
- The page gives one aggregate comparison of 8,088 offers to white candidates and 386 to Black candidates.

These are the article’s report of the Newsnight FOI analysis, not a locally reanalysed dataset. Denominators, institution-level tables, response bias, offer definitions, and the underlying FOI returns remain unavailable here.

## Arday-attributed passages

- The article identifies Arday as an associate professor at Durham and reports his recollection that, although he saw many Black and minority-ethnic students at university, he was never taught by a person of colour.
- He says that supervisors and lecturers selected students for progression to PhD study and that, in his view, the students “cherry-picked” were white and middle-class rather than Black or minority ethnic. This is his account and interpretation of selection practices.
- He says he held several jobs to pay for his degree, that many peers secured doctoral funding, and that he did not know the resource was available; he describes funding information as inaccessible to prospective Black and minority-ethnic academics. These are first-person recollections and policy interpretation.
- He argues that universities present a veneer of egalitarianism, fairness, equality, and diversity while facilitating inequity, and says academics should use their influence to challenge structural inequality. These are his public positions, not findings independently established by the article.

## Source boundary

The article is a dated broadcast-journalism source and a new source-version of Arday’s public account of academic access. It supports what the BBC reported and what it attributed to him; it does not independently verify his employment chronology, funding history, selection experience, or the truth of every FOI-based inference. The article’s data should not be merged with Arday-authored HESA analyses without checking definitions and populations.
