# Cambridge Student interview — page audit

This audit expands the canonical *Cambridge Student* interview record without creating a second interview. The current page carries an explicit notice that its language and tone were revised after discussion with Jason Arday. The Internet Archive captures already contain that revision notice and a `dateModified` value of 4 December 2023; the pre-revision text was not recovered. The page is therefore a revised journalistic source-version, not an unchanged contemporaneous transcript.

## Distinctive subject-centred passages

- **Institutional agenda:** The page attributes to Arday a view that the number of Black professors is a “stain on the sector” and a consequence of institutional racism, and says Cambridge and higher education need to modernise for accessibility and different ways of being and knowing.
- **Slavery legacies:** It records his position that Cambridge should work with Black students, Black staff, and Black historians on reparative acts, including apologies and fiscal reparation, and notes his praise for Jesus College’s work around restitution of Benin Bronzes. These are attributed positions and reported institutional context, not a finding about Cambridge’s legal or historical liability.
- **Simon Woolley narrative:** Arday recounts meeting Woolley through a selective parliamentary scheme for the next generation of Black politicians in 2017, and describes Woolley’s encouragement before broadcast interviews. The page names the scheme but supplies no roster or administrative record; the account remains first-person testimony, with publication-biography corroboration of the scheme name tracked separately.
- **Belonging and assimilation:** Arday says people should be able to enter Cambridge as themselves and frames enforced assimilation as undermining freedom and belonging. This is a direct public-position record, not evidence of every student’s experience.
- **Education and outreach:** He calls education a vehicle for social mobility, supports purposeful outreach, and describes Cambridge’s Foundation Year as promising while arguing that more can be done. These are recommendations and evaluations attributed to him, not an outcome study.
- **Selection and race:** The page attributes to him the view that appointment mechanisms and narrow selection criteria disadvantage ethnic-minority academics, particularly Black women, and records his discussion of normalized casual racism and a Cambridge-linked survey figure that 45% of Black Britons did not feel proud to be British. The underlying survey and causal claims require separate source records.

The page also repeats familiar diagnosis, speech, literacy, charity, and Cambridge-biography material. Those repetitions remain linked to existing claims and are not duplicated here.

## Evidence boundary

The page is journalism built from an interview and editorial framing. The revised wording, the page’s reported institutional context, and Arday’s attributed positions can be recorded; they do not independently verify his childhood, the parliamentary scheme, Cambridge’s history, the survey statistic, or the effectiveness of proposed reforms. The page’s historical “incoming Fellow” wording is preserved as a dated source-version and must not be used as current appointment evidence.
