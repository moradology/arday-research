# Douglass comparison: Vygotsky/MKO passage

## Scope and materials

The `Arday - Texas A&M University source.docx` table in the preserved `plag3.zip` maps Arday thesis pp. 310–311 to the Douglass, Smith, and Smith 2013 article, *An Exploration of the Characteristics of Effective Undergraduate Peer-Mentoring Relationships*, pp. 219–234. The table’s “verbatim” label is a dossier-author characterization, not an institutional finding. The locally preserved HKU conference PDF is a public source-version/precursor associated with the published article; the publisher version remains a separate inaccessible manifestation.

## Direct comparison

Arday p. 310 opens its Vygotsky discussion by saying that social learning theory attributes cognitive development to social interaction between a learner and a more knowledgeable other (MKO), then defines the MKO as someone with greater understanding of a concept, including peers and other students. The conference PDF’s theoretical-construct passage uses the same Vygotsky attribution, the same MKO abbreviation, the same definition sequence, and the same inclusion of peers and other students.

Arday continues that trust facilitates social interaction and that peer mentoring creates an atmosphere in which student teachers can seek assistance from a more knowledgeable peer. The conference source likewise says social interaction is central to peer mentoring and creates an atmosphere in which students feel comfortable seeking assistance from a more knowledgeable peer. Arday adds trust, student-teacher framing, and later Relational-Cultural Theory material, while the comparator develops its own program context.

This is a close, multi-sentence textual and structural correspondence, stronger than a shared citation or general theoretical theme. It is nevertheless a source-version comparison: the conference PDF is not silently treated as byte-identical to the 2013 published article, and the overlap does not by itself establish intent, authorship history, quotation compliance, or misconduct.
