# Arasite final grid — bounded audit of the self-reflection segment

The `ARDAY FINAL GRID.docx` inside SRC-386’s `plag4.zip` maps Arday printed pp. 65–66 to Zwozdiak-Myers printed pp. 47–49. Its row labels (“verbatim,” “close paraphrase,” and similar terms) are dossier-author characterizations, not institutional findings.

## Direct check

The preserved PDFs show the same ordered exposition across this segment. Both texts move from Price’s reported challenges for student teachers—time to reflect, data collection, and mentor support—to practitioner action research and the need to position learning in relation to professional enquiry. They then use Guba, Anderson et al., Kincheloe, McNiff, Stringer, and Usher in a closely related sequence before turning to Schon’s personal epistemology and theories-in-use.

Both passages next discuss Grimmett et al.’s ontological account of self-reflection and the relationship between theory and practice, then develop the continuity of reflective-practitioner growth through Rodgers and Raider-Roth, Holstein and Gubrium, Kagan, Dewey, Lipka and Brinthaupt, Zembylas, Belenky et al.’s “connected teaching,” Bruner, and related teaching/learning reflection. They conclude with Elliott’s self-study/identity formulation, Ellis and Bochner’s account of personal experience, Pollard’s reflective conversations, and Palmer’s “inner self,” personal values, and resistance to standardized teaching expectations.

Arday changes wording, substitutes or adds citations, and adds study-specific concepts such as peer mentoring, communities of practice, and professional development. Nevertheless, the sequence of topics, named authorities, conceptual transitions, and several distinctive formulations are repeatedly retained or closely reworked. This is an extended textual/structural relationship, not a claim that every sentence is verbatim and not a determination of plagiarism, intent, or institutional responsibility.
