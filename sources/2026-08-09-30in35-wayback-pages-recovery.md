# Additional archived campaign pages

The Wayback CDX enumeration preserved under `SRC-377` listed successful 25 August 2010 captures for pages 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 of the 30 in 35 WordPress site. Those six page-level HTML responses are now recovered locally with headers and plain-text extractions. Together with the home page and page 4 already captured under `SRC-377`, they provide a substantially wider contemporaneous campaign snapshot.

## Material recovered

- Page 2 contains the 18 August Q&A with Jason Arday, final-run instructions for Sunday 22 August, a post for marathon 29 (5 hours 7 minutes), earlier marathon 27 and 28 posts, and a campaign-side Facebook feed stating: “Marathon 30 - 5 hrs 57 min - an amazing end to an amazing event!”
- Page 3 contains marathon 23–26 reports, with stated times from 5 hours 9 minutes to 6 hours 3 minutes, and a “Fundraising target smashed!” post. It also repeats the invitation to join the final marathon on 22 August.
- Page 5 contains marathon 14–18 reports, a fundraising update, and a page post reproducing Ranulph Fiennes’s recorded encouragement to Arday and the beneficiary charities.
- Page 6 contains marathon 11–13 reports, including stated times of 4 hours 38 minutes, 4 hours 42 minutes, and 4 hours 49 minutes; it records an interim fundraising total of £4,520 and says a *Runner’s World* interview was due.
- Page 7 contains marathon 6–10 reports, with stated times from 4 hours 29 minutes to 4 hours 46 minutes, plus contemporaneous fundraising encouragement for Shelter and Shooting Star.
- Page 8 contains marathon 3–5 reports, stated times of 4 hours 33 minutes, 4 hours 37 minutes, and 4 hours 39 minutes, and a fundraising update saying the total had exceeded £4,000.

The pages are contemporaneous campaign records and include first-person posts, campaign comments, and embedded social-feed material. They materially strengthen the record that the campaign represented the marathons as being run through number 30, including a final time, but they remain campaign-side evidence rather than an independent race-timing log, charity receipt, or audited account. The “Marathon 30” statement appears in the archived page’s embedded Facebook feed; it is preserved as a source-version and not silently upgraded to a separate Facebook source.

## Evidence boundary

These captures establish the campaign’s dated narrative, reported marathon numbers and times, interim fundraising figures, final-run logistics, and the campaign’s representation of completion. They do not independently verify route distances, elapsed times, all 30 starts/finishes, final proceeds, or the employment and biography claims repeated in the Q&A. The posts should be read alongside the contemporaneous *Tes* report, sponsor release, JustGiving records, and later institutional biographies.
