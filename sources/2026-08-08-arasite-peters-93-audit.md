# Peters–Le Cornu–Collins comparison: Arday p. 93

## Scope and materials

The `Aderibigbe-Christie-Le Cornu.docx` table in the preserved `plag2.zip` maps Arday thesis p. 93 to page 3 of Judy Peters, Rosie Le Cornu, and Janet Collins’s November 2003 report *Towards Constructivist Teaching and Learning*. The report is identified by the South Australian government’s indexed PDF manifestation as a 21-page report produced with the Learning to Learn Project. The direct government PDF request returned HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare managed challenge on 8 August 2026; no local report PDF is claimed.

## Indexed-text comparison

The indexed report text states that “central to constructivism” is the active role of learners in constructing their own meaning, then describes social constructivism through the learning environment and social interaction. On its next page it says that learning should be “participatory, proactive, communal, collaborative” and given over to constructing meanings rather than receiving them, attributing that formulation to Bruner (1996, p. 84).

Arday p. 93 uses the same distinctive “central to constructivism” framing, the active construction of meaning, and the social-constructivist reference to social interaction. It then reproduces the same ordered Bruner adjective sequence, changing the report’s “given over to” wording to “submissive to,” and adds a discussion of student and in-service teachers in learning communities. Arday attributes the surrounding sentence to Kensington-Miller (2011), not to Peters–Le Cornu–Collins; both texts explicitly attribute the distinctive adjective sequence to Bruner (1996, p. 84).

This is a real bounded textual relationship, including a shared distinctive sequence and conceptual order. The common Bruner attribution is material provenance context: it leaves open whether the overlap came through Bruner, the 2003 report, another intermediary, or a combination. The exhibit’s “verbatim text” label is therefore retained as source-attributed characterization, not adopted as a conclusion about the whole paragraph.
