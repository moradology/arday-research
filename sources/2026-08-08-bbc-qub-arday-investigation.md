# BBC News NI — Queen’s University Belfast investigation report

Robbie Meredith’s BBC News NI report, published 8 August 2026, says that Queen’s University Belfast (QUB) is investigating work it published that was co-authored by Jason Arday and QUB academics. The report identifies the paper as the 2021 article [*Attempting to break the chain: Reimaging inclusive pedagogy and decolonising the curriculum within the academy*](2020-attempting-break-chain-inclusive-pedagogy.md), already canonical in this archive.

The report quotes a QUB spokesperson: “We are currently looking into the matter and liaising with other UK universities in relation to it.” This is a direct, named institutional response as reported by the BBC. It does not state the scope, procedure, allegations under review, investigator, timetable, interim measures, or outcome. It is not itself a finding of plagiarism, fabrication, or misconduct.

The BBC describes the paper as drawing partly on interviews with students and staff, reproducing two participant quotations, and recommending a less Eurocentric and more inclusive university curriculum. Those are article-description and primary-text-context claims; the article itself remains the source for its methods, quotations, and recommendations. The report also says Arday has admitted errors in his work but denied plagiarism and attributed some mistakes to autism and inadequate early supervision; this is reported self-defense, not an adjudication.
