# Scope

This is a bounded audit of Professor Jason Arday’s contribution to Anglia Ruskin University’s 2 April 2025 Unlearn/Lightbulb recording, “I Guess I’m Doing Fine, I Guess I’m Doing Fine.” The host introductions and later audience/Q&A material were not treated as Arday’s speech. The local MP4 is 1:35:35.02; the public page exposes no caption track.

## Arday-attributed navigation

The following windows are approximate navigation from a machine-assisted English pass over the local audio. They are not verbatim quotations and should be checked against the waveform before quotation.

- **approximately 10:33–12:20:** Arday uses music and sport as common-ground devices, describes belonging in a neurodivergent context as fluid and action-oriented, and says his aim is to talk with people rather than at them.
- **approximately 12:20–14:59:** he characterises academia as an unkind space in which selfishness, vanity, individualism, and exploitation can be rewarded; he connects exclusion to class, gender, sexuality, religion, race, and disability; and he contrasts theory without action with praxis, collaboration, and stakeholder work.
- **approximately 15:00–18:00:** he frames inclusion as the ordinary wish to be seen, valued, nurtured, supported, and to belong; he asks who sets the rules of institutional membership and recommends small acts within one’s sphere of influence as a way to reject exclusion.
- **approximately 18:00–22:00:** he links precariousness in higher education to belonging, wellness, mental health, job security, and the experience of neurodivergent people in a neurotypical world. He recounts challenging an unnamed colleague’s ableist and racialised disparagement of another person, presenting self-questioning and responsibility as part of occupying a position of representation.
- **approximately 22:00–25:00:** he presents collectivism and “being together” as instruments for workplace freedom and belonging, extends the discussion beyond neurodiversity to broader institutional membership, and closes this bounded pass with an expressed belief in human beings’ capacity for collective change.
