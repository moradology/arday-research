# BAME/BME definition footnote audit

## Scope and materials

The Arasite note for *Fighting the tide* points out that its explanation of the term BAME follows the same format as another Arday article. This audit checks only that footnote relationship:

- *Fighting the tide* PDF page 7, footnote 1, uses “Black and Minority Ethnic” and the abbreviation “BAME.”
- *No one can see me cry* accepted-manuscript PDF page 2, footnote 1, uses “black and minority ethnic” and the abbreviation “BME.”

The two underlying public PDFs were inspected directly. The colored or evaluative framing in the Arasite note is not adopted as a conclusion.

## Direct comparison

The footnotes share the same uncommon sequence and near-identical sentence structure: a recommendation to use precise ethnic descriptions; a statement that the paper will use the relevant collective term and acronym for people other than white British; a parenthetical list including Black African, African Caribbean, Asian, Latin-American, and other minority communities; an acknowledgement that the term is not universally accepted and has limitations; a statement that it is used in research and government papers; and a closing explanation that it is used descriptively because it was preferred by most participants.

The visible changes are systematic but limited: BAME becomes BME, capitalization differs, “white British” and surrounding wording are formatted differently, and the cited names use `&` in *Fighting the tide* versus “and” in *No one can see me cry*. The page-level evidence therefore supports a close textual relationship between the two footnotes. It does not establish whether the same author reused an earlier template, whether an intermediate source or editorial process was involved, whether reuse was disclosed or permitted, or whether any institutional standard was breached.

## Interpretation boundary

This is a narrow primary-text observation. It is not a finding that either article contains fabricated data, plagiarism, or research misconduct. The two articles remain separate publications, and the comparison does not extend to adjacent prose or to other articles’ BME/BAME definitions.

The 2018 article is now checked as the earlier lineage instance in [SRC-473](2026-08-08-arasite-bame-footnote-lineage-audit.md). That extension does not change this record’s two-paper scope.
