# BBC Radio 4 *Beyond Lonely* series

The BBC’s current playable-episode API exposes three surviving records from the five-part *Beyond Lonely* series, presented by Jason Arday in April 2025. The series-level synopsis repeats the public biographical wording that Arday was diagnosed with autism and global developmental delay as a child, learned to speak at eleven, and learned to read and write at eighteen. This is BBC programme metadata, not an independent medical or school record.

## Current episode records

- “A Lonely Life” (`m0029zdd`) — 14 April 2025; synopsis describes Arday’s loneliness and personal history.
- “Young and Lonely in the Connected World” (`m0029zbb`) — 15 April 2025; synopsis says that as a teenager Arday spent hours practising snooker shots, valued the focus and solitude of the snooker hall, and now connects that experience to loneliness.
- “Ever So Lonely” (`m0029yxk`) — 16 April 2025; synopsis discusses work, lockdown, isolation, and his tendency to lock himself away while writing.

Two additional episode IDs found in contemporary listings, `m0029zng` and `m0029zfx`, returned current BBC Sounds 404 pages on 6 August 2026. The API labels the surviving records’ downloads as DRM and exposes no file URLs, so no audio binary is claimed.

## Earlier listing record

BBC Radio 4 listings identify Jason Arday as presenter of “All the Lonely People,” an episode considering loneliness, his mother Gifty’s encouragement, and conversations with people including Amy Perrin of the Marmalade Trust. The contemporary listing dates this episode to 17 April 2025; the current BBC API exposes a different three-episode subset, so this earlier record is retained as a superseded/changed availability state rather than silently merged.

## Evidence boundary

- Broadcast date: 17 April 2025, within the 12–18 April schedule.
- Producer: Maggie Ayre.
- The programme listing supplies a synopsis and presenter credit, not a transcript.
- On 2026-08-06 the prior canonical BBC programme URL returned HTTP 404. The DRM/download boundary and 404 responses are preserved as changed-state evidence; no local audio is claimed.
