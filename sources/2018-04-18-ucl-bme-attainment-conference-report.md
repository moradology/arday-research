## Key facts reported by UCL

- UCL says its first BME Attainment Conference took place at the Institute of Education on 10 April 2018.
- The article identifies Jason Arday as guest speaker, then Senior Lecturer in Education Studies at Roehampton University and a UCL alumnus.
- UCL attributes to Arday the argument that four decades of interventions had relied on a deficit model focused on students’ perceived weaknesses rather than persistent racism and institutional structures.
- UCL attributes to Arday the statement: “If you can’t see yourself reflected in the curricula, it’s going to affect attainment,” alongside a first-person account of ten years in higher education without being taught by a person of colour.
- The article reports next steps including an inclusive-curriculum health check, faculty leads, a resource bank, and unconscious-bias training.
- The article originally stated a 5.8% 2016–17 attainment gap, but also carries a note that the figure was changed on 13 May 2019 because of data discrepancies. Treat that percentage as a corrected/unstable historical page claim, not as independently validated data.

## Evidence boundary

The article is strong evidence of what UCL reported after the event and of the public framing attributed to Arday. It is not a transcript, independent audit of the underlying attainment data, or proof that every listed institutional next step was implemented. The keynote PDF remains the separate event artifact; do not merge the two records or count the article as a second delivery.
