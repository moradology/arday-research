# *The Broken Pipeline*

Leading Routes published this 2019 report with Williams, Bath, Arday, and Lewis. It examines barriers facing Black PhD students seeking Research Council funding.

## Evidence boundary

- Durham records the publication year as 2019 and the publisher as Leading Routes, London.
- The two URLs are one report’s publisher and repository records.
- The report is a public-facing policy/roundtable report, not a peer-reviewed article or a transparent population survey.

## Page-level extraction

- The first-party PDF is an eight-page September 2019 report by Paulette Williams, Sukhi Bath, Jason Arday, and Chantelle Lewis (cover).
- It records a closed February 2019 roundtable convened by Leading Routes with sector professionals, students, academics, higher-education institutions, and research-funding representatives. The stated purpose was to discuss factors influencing Black students’ experience of securing council-funded postgraduate research places and possible collaborative responses (p. 3).
- The report presents contextual figures attributed to HESA and a UKRI Freedom of Information response, including 15,560 first-year full-time UK-domiciled PhD students in 2017/18 with 3% identified as Black, and 245 of 19,868 UKRI-funded studentships across 2016/17–2018/19 awarded to Black or Black Mixed students (30 to Black Caribbean students) (p. 3). These are cited external statistics, not a dataset collected by the report.
- Its discussion identifies prior-attainment emphasis, preference for research-intensive institutions, limited applicant data and conversion evidence, scholarship-eligibility concerns, variable application information, fixed notions of academic excellence, and potential bias/microaggressions in one-to-one supervision as factors raised in the roundtable (pp. 5–6).
- Recommendations include better applicant-level data, research into Black students’ funded-PGR experiences, broader definitions of academic ability, diverse interview panels, clearer ownership of admissions, paid research experience, transparent guidance, possible ring-fenced funding, and team supervision (p. 5).
- The final section records Leading Routes’ planned 2019–20 responses—events, two paid Wellcome summer-internship places, further research, consultancy, a YouTube/podcast series, network support, and a Black in Academia festival (p. 6). These are stated commitments, not proof of delivery or impact.
