# SRC-468 — Arasite `plag1` limitations and reflective-practice comparisons

The public `plag1.zip` contains `More Z-M - summaries(1).docx`, a comparison table that maps passages in Arday’s 2015 thesis to Paula Zwozdiak-Myers’s 2009 Brunel thesis. This record audits the distinct examples in that file; it does not re-count the already audited screenshot or the `plag4` ranges recorded under SRC-405–SRC-444.

## Direct comparison

The first `plag1` block maps Arday’s printed p. 343 to the Zwozdiak-Myers limitations passage beginning “While insights have been gained.” In the preserved PDFs this is Arday PDF p. 343 and Zwozdiak-Myers PDF p. 279 (the comparator page visibly carries printed p. 268). Both passages frame limitations in a qualitative reflective-practice study, discuss uncertainty about whether development resulted from the intervention, and then move into data gathered from participant perceptions. Arday changes several words and expands the passage; the relationship is more than a shared topic or generic methodology language, but it is not one uninterrupted verbatim block.

The next rows map Arday’s printed pp. 343–344 to Zwozdiak-Myers’s source-labelled pp. 269 and 269–270. The preserved pages show close correspondence in the interpretation/bias discussion and in the paragraph beginning “Evidence gathered to inform this study relied upon the research participants’ understanding…”. The latter paragraph is nearly sentence-for-sentence in structure and wording, with Arday inserting “and disadvantages,” changing “power relations” to “organic bias” in the adjacent row, and adding or changing surrounding methodological detail.

A separate row maps Arday’s printed pp. 346–347 to Zwozdiak-Myers’s source-labelled p. 265 (PDF p. 276, visibly printed p. 265). Both passages state that student teachers recognise the interdependent relationship between reflection and self-study/peer mentoring, recommend ongoing professional development, invoke Zeichner and Liston’s disposition/skills formulation, and describe action research as a point of departure for later practice. Arday changes “self study” to “peer-mentoring” in the surrounding framing and changes “internalise” to “verbalise” in the quoted-attributed formulation; the sequence and several distinctive phrases remain closely aligned.

The final `plag1` row maps Arday’s printed p. 350 to Zwozdiak-Myers’s source-labelled pp. 270–271 (PDF pp. 281–282, visibly printed pp. 270–271). Both present the same three recommendations: replicate with primary/secondary physical-education student teachers, replicate in other subject areas, and use a larger sample to support broader generalisation. Arday expands the wording and changes “helpful”/“beneficial” phrasing, but retains the ordered recommendation set.

These observations establish bounded textual and structural relationships in the specified pages. They do not establish intent, authorship history, an intermediate source, quotation/citation compliance, permission, plagiarism, or an institutional research-integrity finding. The `plag1` labels such as “verbatim text” and “close paraphrase” remain attributed dossier characterizations.
