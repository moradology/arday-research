# *How Do You Cope?* — *The Gratitude List* audio audit

This is a bounded navigation pass over the 21:13.29 locally preserved bonus episode. ## Arday-attributed first-person windows

- **01:21–05:29** — Arday explains his constant emphasis on gratitude by describing his upbringing as “profoundly poor,” a family maxim that charity begins at home, and his father’s account of a possible “beauty” in poverty. He says his mother and brothers practised daily gratitude for food, clothes, love, and community, and that this shaped gratitude as something embodied rather than merely stated. These are retrospective family and socioeconomic self-reports.
- **02:49–05:29** — He gives a hotel-room noise as an example of deliberately placing an irritation beside the larger fact of having shelter, a bed, and provision. He says his default can be fault-finding and annoyance, so gratitude is a conscious corrective. This is self-description and ethical reflection, not a psychological assessment.
- **07:14–10:48** — Arday identifies his mother as the person to whom he is most grateful and says she tried many ways to engage him, develop socialisation, and build understanding. He describes her guiding principle as “feeding the faith and starving the fear,” and recounts an age-eight episode in which she taught him to eat an apple over several years before he understood it. This is first-person testimony and remembered family dialogue, not an independently documented developmental or clinical record.
- **12:11–13:40** — He says the Cambridge-professor identity is not the whole of him and is not what he wishes to be defined by; he recalls that his mother would have been satisfied with any adult path if he tried his best and became able to stand on his own feet. This records self-conception and remembered maternal values.
- **14:11–17:56** — Arday says many people’s small acts of kindness helped carry him to his present position. He recalls his mother teaching him the “Footprints” poem at 21 and says he has read it daily for nearly 20 years. The passage is a personal interpretive framework and recollection, not evidence of a complete support network or independently verified reading practice.
- **18:37–20:49** — He says music was central to his development and that his mother used it as a medium for understanding language. He identifies Enya’s “Caribbean Blue,” musical instruments, and imagined/ethereal sound as tools his mother used to cultivate imagination; he describes himself as very literal as a child and places this experimentation in the late 1980s. These are first-person accounts of family strategy and childhood experience, not evidence of treatment efficacy or a general claim about autistic children.
