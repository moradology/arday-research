# Jason Arday — working biography

This is a source-led biography. It keeps the narrative readable while marking which details are directly documented, self-reported, repeated by institutions, or still require corroboration.

The EDICa project’s current team page lists Arday as a co-investigator and describes his research areas as race and higher education, intersectionality and education, mental health and education, neurodiversity, and cultural studies. The page is valuable first-party evidence for that project-role edge and preserves a linked portrait, but its present-tense affiliation biography is dated host copy and does not override the later Cambridge resignation or separate legal/host records. [EDICa team record](sources/2026-08-09-edica-team-jason-arday.md)

The January 2021 London Metropolitan University/Centre for Equity and Inclusion report *Institutionalised: The Rise of Islamophobia in Higher Education* contains Arday’s foreword, “A New Dawn: Addressing Islamophobia in British Higher Education.” The foreword argues that Islamophobia is institutional racism and calls for university interventions; the report itself was produced by Sofia Akel and reports separate surveys of 93 students and 13 staff. Arday is not treated as the empirical report author, and the report’s findings are not back-attributed to him. [Institutionalised page audit](sources/2026-08-09-institutionalised-report-page-audit.md)

[Video-bearing event record](sources/2024-03-27-cambridge-festival-race-society-radio.md)

The most defensible account of Arday at this stage is layered rather than singular. His public identity, appointments, publications, media appearances, and several community/club records are directly documentable. The childhood, disability, literacy, family, fundraising, and sports narratives are unusually rich but mostly come from Arday’s own interviews, memoir-related material, or institutional biographies repeating those accounts. Repetition increases the evidence that this is the biography he has publicly presented; it does not turn self-report into an independent medical, school, financial, or sporting record.

The June 2020 BBC *Moral Maze* appearance adds a dated public-position layer rather than new biography: Arday argues for collective responsibility for racism, distinguishes formal curriculum lists from classroom implementation and historical framing, describes education as preparation for participation in society, and calls for sustained institutional work rather than seasonal symbolic action. These are his recorded arguments, preserved separately from empirical claims about racism, curriculum effects, or institutional outcomes. ## One-paragraph overview

The Autism Research Centre’s Autistic VOICES study page, published in March 2026 and modified in June, lists Arday on the project’s Steering Committee. The public project aims to identify unmet needs of autistic university students and plans a co-designed survey of 500–3,000 students, with results anticipated in 2027. This verifies a named research-service role and project topology, not authorship, principal-investigator status, funding, or results. [Autistic VOICES record](sources/2026-03-11-autistic-voices-study-arday.md)

A January 2026 Cambridge PhD thesis by Sasha Henriques names Arday alongside Anna Middleton as one of the project’s two PhD supervisors in participant-information appendices (PDF pp. 308, 312). This is direct first-party evidence of a supervision role in the thesis materials; it does not establish the appointment date, examination outcome, full scope of supervision, or continuation after Arday’s later resignation. [Henriques thesis](sources/2026-07-09-cambridge-repository-henriques-thesis.md)

Jason Arday is a British sociologist of education whose public work has focused on race and inequality in higher education, mental health, neurodiversity, intersectionality, and decolonising curricula. The official *Cambridge University Reporter* records that he was elected Professor of Education (2002) with effect from 6 March 2023, after identifying him as Professor of Sociology of Education at the University of Glasgow; later Cambridge and contemporary media records describe the appointment as Professor of Sociology of Education. He had earlier academic roles at Durham and Glasgow. His biography has received substantial public attention because institutional profiles and interviews describe a childhood involving autism and global developmental delay, delayed speech and literacy, a strong involvement in football and snooker, later higher education study, academic work, and extensive fundraising. A Norfolk specialist school separately named an autism-resource classroom after him and, in an additional self-evaluation source-version, records that he visited the school, met children, and spoke to SEND parents; this is institutional reception evidence, not independent verification of the underlying biography. [Drake self-evaluation audit](sources/2026-08-09-drake-arday-class.md) · [Cambridge Reporter election audit](sources/2026-08-09-cambridge-reporter-election-page-audit.md)

## Early life and communication

Institutional and broadcast profiles state that Arday was diagnosed with autism and global developmental delay at around age three, and that he did not speak until age eleven. The public record also contains a later trade-review formulation saying he did not speak until twelve, while the publisher page for his forthcoming memoir says he spent “almost twelve years” without language. The Liverpool John Moores profile says his family were told he might need lifelong support. PBS describes his first spoken word as “hello” at age eleven after extensive speech therapy; he says spoken-language development continued for years afterward. This archive records the competing formulations rather than silently resolving them.

A 28 November 2023 interview published in Portuguese by *Revista História Hoje* adds a fuller first-person account of family and childhood context. Arday describes Ghanaian and South African parents, severe family poverty, parents who fed and hosted people with little money, childhood visits with his mother to homeless shelters, and being deaf in one ear. He connects the absence of speech to observing and listening. The interview also recounts an age-18 shelter encounter that he says shaped his charity mission, and names Sandro’s “will versus ability” lesson and Black-feminist intellectual influences. It also records his positions on praxis, community knowledge, strategic composure, meritocracy, and educational change. Because the article is translated and edited, these are preserved as attributed autobiographical testimony and public-position statements—not independent medical, family, shelter, charity, or institutional records. [RHHJ interview](sources/2024-12-11-rhhj-jason-arday-interview.md) · [Page audit](sources/2026-08-09-rhhj-interview-page-audit.md)

The March 2023 *Neurodivergent Show* interview provides another, more granular autobiographical version of the early-life narrative. Arday describes removing a hearing aid while practising exercises, repeating “hello” after his mother’s prompting at age eleven, and understanding the moment as a conversion of unfavourable odds through perseverance. He places himself at eighteen with a reported reading age of eight and recounts Sandro Sandri urging him to think beyond supermarket work and toward university. These are automatic-caption-derived first-person claims; the hearing, literacy, diagnostic, and mentor details remain unverified by clinical, assessment, family, or college records. [Caption audit](sources/2026-08-09-neurodivergent-show-caption-audit.md)

Because this conflicts in emphasis with the special-school-to-mainstream wording and remains unsupported by school or sporting records, both versions are retained. A 2023 secondary commentary account names the mainstream school as Southfields Community College, now Southfields Academy, and reports two GCSEs in PE and textiles; the school’s own history page confirms the Community College-to-Academy name transition but gives no Arday attendance or examination record. The current South Thames Colleges Group directly identifies him as a former Merton College student and names Sandro Sandri as a sports lecturer and mentor there. Its dated profile also records the education-to-Cambridge sequence as the institution presented it in March 2023; this is host biography and source-version evidence, not a student file, qualification certificate, or current-status record. Cambridge’s 2023 appointment biography says his mother used music to support language and confidence, while Sandri later supported his reading, writing, and academic ambitions. These are reported or attributed life-history accounts, not school, medical, or family records.
