    On July 4th, the United States marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence. “The 1619 Project” traces a longer timeline, one that begins 157 years earlier, in August 1619, when some 20 to 30 captive Africans arrived in Virginia, becoming the first enslaved people in the English colonies that would become the United States. The Pulitzer Center’s “Defining Democracy” workshop series has been equipping educators with resources and strategies for exploring the history and future of democracy through “The 1619 Project.” The “Understanding American Democracy” toolkit, developed by Pulitzer Center's Donnalie Jamnah, brings together teaching materials from that series and is free for educators everywhere.     As President of the New Collar Association of South Africa (NCASA), I welcome the upcoming UNESCO Transforming Education Summit +4 taking place on 10 July 2026. Four years after the landmark Transforming Education Summit, the world is once again reflecting on one of the most critical questions of our time: How do we prepare people for the future of work? The answer lies in recognising that education can no longer be confined to traditional academic pathways alone. The rise of the New Collar Economy is reshaping labour markets globally, creating opportunities that prioritise skills, competencies, digital literacy, innovation and lifelong learning over conventional qualifications. Across South Africa and the African continent, millions of young people are entering a rapidly evolving workforce driven by Artificial Intelligence, digital transformation, green technologies, advanced manufacturing, entrepreneurship and the platform economy. To remain competitive, education systems must evolve from knowledge transfer to skills development, adaptability and employability. The UNESCO Transforming Education Summit +4 presents an important opportunity for governments, educational institutions, industry leaders and civil society to strengthen collaboration and accelerate reforms that ensure education remains relevant, inclusive and future-focused. At NCASA, we strongly advocate for: ✅ Skills-first pathways to employment ✅ Industry-aligned education and training ✅ Digital and AI literacy for all learners ✅ Increased opportunities for women and youth in emerging industries ✅ Recognition of micro-credentials and alternative learning pathways ✅ Stronger public-private partnerships to bridge the skills gap The future belongs to those who can continuously learn, adapt and innovate. Education systems must therefore become engines of economic participation, social mobility and sustainable development. As we look towards Summit +4, let us commit to building an education ecosystem that empowers every learner to thrive in the New Collar Economy and contribute meaningfully to achieving the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. The future of work is already here.
