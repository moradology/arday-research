Glasgow University denies Jason Arday visiting prof claim

 
Exclusive by Andrew Learmonth Political Editor

Share

0 Comments
People walking among the cloisters beneath Bute Hall at the University of Glasgow. (Image: Colin Mearns)
This article is brought to you by our exclusive subscriber partnership with our sister title USA Today, and has been written by our American colleagues. It does not necessarily reflect the view of The Herald.

The University of Glasgow has told The Herald that Professor Jason Arday, the academic embroiled in a growing plagiarism row, has never held a visiting professorship there, directly contradicting his official biography on Cambridge's website.

In 2021, Prof Arday became a Professor of Sociology of Education at the University of Glasgow’s School of Education, making him, at the time, one of the youngest professors in the UK.

He moved to Cambridge in 2023.

Read More: Jason Arday quits Cambridge University over plagiarism row

The Cambridge Faculty of Education page, which forms part of his profile as a Fellow at Jesus College and holder of the 2002 Professorial Chair in the Sociology of Education, claims he still has a relationship with Glasgow.

It states he is currently "a Visiting Professor at The Ohio State University in the Office of Diversity and Inclusion, University of Glasgow in the School of Education, and an Honorary Professor at Durham University in the Department of Sociology".

However, when asked if that was accurate, a spokesperson for Glasgow said: "I can confirm that Professor Arday isn't a Visiting Professor at the University of Glasgow."

When asked if he had ever held that position, the spokesperson said he "was never a Visiting Professor here".

The revelation adds a new dimension to a row that has engulfed Prof Arday over the past week.

Nathan Cofnas, an academic sacked from a Cambridge college role in 2024, said he had run Prof Arday's PhD thesis through plagiarism detection software and found what he described as passages "lifted with minimal editing, sometimes retaining copy-editing mistakes from the original source".

The Telegraph subsequently reported that more than 100 passages in the thesis were said to closely resemble an earlier thesis by another academic, Paula Zwozdiak-Myers of Brunel University London.

Prof Arday has not disputed that errors exist in his work. He has instead argued that he lacked adequate supervision during his PhD because of his autism and learning disabilities, and could not afford a proofreader early in his career.

(Image: Cambridge University)

He told the Times he was accountable for those mistakes but that "what I am not is a liar".

Cambridge has defended Prof Arday, saying investigations into academic misconduct are, as standard practice, carried out by the institution where the research took place — in this case Liverpool John Moores University, which the Guardian reported had reviewed the thesis and found no plagiarism.

The row has also extended to Prof Arday's public claims about charity fundraising challenges.

He has said he raised more than five million pounds for charity over 20 years, including through running 30 marathons in 35 days and 600 miles in six days.

He has since told the Guardian the 600-mile challenge was actually completed over 12 days, with rest days built in, and that two universities which awarded him honorary degrees — Southampton Solent and Anglia Ruskin — have amended their websites to remove or correct the claims.

He said he could not name those who helped raise the £5m because of non-disclosure agreements.

It is unclear how long the Glasgow claims have appeared on the Cambridge website, or whether Cambridge relied on information supplied by Prof Arday himself in compiling the profile.

Champions Speakers, the agency that books the academic for events, also lists the claims on its website.
