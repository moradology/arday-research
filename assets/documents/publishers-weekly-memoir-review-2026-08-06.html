Great and Unfortunate Things: A Memoir

Jason Arday, with Eve Claxton. 37 Ink, $29 (288p) ISBN 978-1-6680-8557-8

Sociologist Arday’s inspiring debut illuminates his evolution from nonspeaking autistic child to one of the youngest academics ever appointed to the University of Cambridge. Born in 1985, Arday was diagnosed with autism and developmental delays at age three and did not speak until he was 12. He recalls enduring barbed taunts from classmates and demeaning diagnoses from doctors (“There’s no one in there”) while struggling academically well into adolescence. A turning point arrived in early adulthood, when an 18-year-old Arday learned to read and write after meeting his future wife, Debs, who introduced him to phonics while they worked together at a sporting goods store in South London. In 2005, Arday earned admission to St. Mary’s College, where he parlayed his love of sports into a degree in physical education. As Arday charts his subsequent path to a career in academia, he pays tribute to the people who shaped his progress, including his mother, Giff, and Angela, a fellow student who helped him take notes during his earliest days at St. Mary’s. Arday’s perseverance and self-belief are undeniably affecting. It’s a stirring story, straightforwardly told. Agent: Jennifer Gates, Aevitas Creative Management. (Aug.)
