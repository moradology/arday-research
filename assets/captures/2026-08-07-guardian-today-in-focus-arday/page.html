Today in FocusJason Arday

Plagiarism and a pig’s head: the rise and fall of Jason Arday – podcast

Jason Arday’s appointment as Cambridge’s youngest black professor made global headlines. Then questions about his meteoric rise began to snowball. Lanre Bakare reports.

More ways to listen

  • Spotify
  • RSS Feed
  • Download
Presented by Helen Pidd and Lanre Bakare, with Eli Block, Alex Atack, Nilufer Aumeerally and Brian McNamara; executive producer Homa Khaleeli
Thu 6 Aug 2026 22.00 EDT
Share

When the University of Cambridge appoints a new professor, it doesn’t usually make global headlines. But Jason Arday was different. He was just 37 in 2023 when he became Cambridge’s youngest black professor, specialising in the sociology of race and education. The achievement seemed all the more remarkable because of his incredible back story.

Born to Ghanaian parents who moved to the UK in the early 1980s, Arday was diagnosed with global development delay and autism spectrum disorder at three years of age. He said he hadn’t learned to speak until he was 11 and that he couldn’t really read and write until he was 18. But he defied all expectations, and as his star rose, he received honours usually reserved for celebrities.

Yet almost as soon as he began his professorship at Cambridge’s Jesus College, the whispers began. Initially, the focus was on his academic scholarship. But, the Guardian’s arts and culture correspondent Lanre Bakare tells Helen Pidd, the allegations snowballed, and soon every aspect of his life story was put under the microscope. On Wednesday night, he resigned.

But how did this happen? And what does this tell us about how black scholars are treated by academia and the media?
