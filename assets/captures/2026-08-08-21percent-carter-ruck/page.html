Who paid for Carter-Ruck …

Published by 21percent.org on 28 July 202628 July 2026

The story at Retraction Watch here is the most damaging yet.

Once allegations were raised, the issue ceased to be a matter solely for the individual academic. It became a test of Cambridge’s commitment to academic integrity and transparency. The involvement of Carter-Ruck, a firm known for reputation management and high-profile litigation, inevitably raises questions about whether the priority was discovering the truth or controlling the damage.

Who paid for Carter-Ruck? If Jason Arday, then he is not an innocent.

If Cambridge University, then this is corruption.

A university’s first instinct when faced with serious concerns about academic integrity should be openness, independent scrutiny and a clear public account of the facts. When the senior leadership instead move quickly towards legal containment, protecting reputations has taken precedence over protecting standards.

That does not by itself prove a deliberate cover-up … but we are now very, very close.

The central questions are unavoidable: who knew what, when did they know it and why was the response not more transparent?

Eileen Nugent · 28 July 2026 at 15:16

If the action of a person scrutinising the work of another person in academia can in itself be classified as bullying/harassing a person then this would make it impossible for academia to function. The fundamental basis of academia is that a persons academic work can be scrutinised by others in academia for authenticity and/or academic rigour. This is an integral part of academia that has persisted throughout the ages and it has not changed in the current age.

AI might temporarily have made it look like the fundamental nature of academia has changed but eventually AI will be reporting on whether it generated a piece of academic work for a person and which pieces of academic work it generated for a person. The temporary state of affairs that currently exists where a person could offload academic work onto AI and pass off work generated by AI as their own will be gone because people using AI in that way – i.e. to not do any academic work – and not in more productive ways – i.e. to refine/check/advance any academic work being done – serves no useful purpose for society.

The fundamental nature of academia has not changed, it is still a space to produce authentic work, a space to produce new knowledge that can be scrutinised by others for authenticity and academic rigour, a space to produce academic work that can be independently verified by others, a space to independently verify the academic work of others in order to make it possible to build on existing academic work with accuracy, a space to solve problems of academic interest that will eventually become problems of real world interest, a space to solve problems currently of real world interest.

Implicit in a person actively participating in academia is a person opening up their own academic work to scrutiny by others actively participating in academia. If a person is not prepared to open their academic work up to scrutiny and to in turn scrutinise the academic work of others in academia then a person is not actively participating in academia. Widening participation in academia means bringing more people in to academia to actively participate in academia, it doesn’t mean bringing more people in to academia to not actively participate in academia.

People vary in their ability to do academic work and therefore in their ability to advance in academia, this is a fact of life, even siblings raised within the same family can have very different abilities to do academic work. It is in the doing of academic work that a person discovers what their ability to do academic work is. Actively participating in something means doing something & seeing what happens in the doing of the something. It means exploring whether it is possible to develop the skill to do something to match the will to do something and to make some progress in the doing of that something. It doesn’t mean that actively participating in something is always going to lead to a continuous state of success in relation to that something and/or to be a pain free experience without any encounters with failure in the doing of that something.

Actively participating in academia means a person being open to what they can achieve in academia, a person remaining in an unbiased state about whether academia/other academics hold any undue bias in relation to them in order to accurately detect any undue bias, a person not being overly biased either for or against themselves in academia, a person remaining open to critical feedback, a person remaining sensitive to their own ability to do academic work and to their own ability to take on critical feedback about their own academic work and to use it to drive improvements in their own academic performance.

It is up to every person to discover what is true of them in relation to academia. Other people can give a person critical feedback but it’s up to the person themselves to work out how to respond to that critical feedback and to work out what is true of them in relation to academia. If a person cannot work out what is true of themselves in academia a person can end up with an inaccurate representation of themselves in relation to academia which over longer timescales could prove unsustainable for a person to maintain as the person continues their academic journey.

Before embarking on the academic journey it’s important for a person to be firm about the value they place in themselves and to hold firm to that value in themselves throughout that academic journey. Care of a person is not conditional on academic success or failure, support for a person is not conditional on academic success or failure. The person comes out the other end of the academic journey whether or not the academic career comes out the other end of the academic journey. On any academic journey there could be significant amounts of failure with no guarantee of eventually success.

If there is new critical feedback available to a person then a measure of a person is how the person responds to that new critical feedback. No two Cambridge academics are alike, it’s up to each Cambridge academic to find their own way of being a Cambridge academic given their own individual set of circumstances, it is up to each Cambridge academic to respond to critical feedback, it is up to each Cambridge academic to maintain trust and confidence with the academic community inside and outside of Cambridge and with the public.

If a person is working at the level of Chair in Cambridge a person can expect a high level of scrutiny from the academic community and from the public. A person working at this level can expect that if there is any controversy in relation to the persons academic work that an internal examination might need to be done and that the internal examination might then need to be opened up to independent external examiners. This is the nature of working at that particular level in Cambridge, this is what active participation in academia looks like at that level in Cambridge. Widening participation in academia means bringing more people in to academia to actively participate in academia at all levels of academia.
