## Safe narrative for the biography

Arday’s public life story places his childhood in Clapham, in a Ghanaian family, and describes autism/global developmental delay, very delayed speech, and literacy acquired around 18. He has also described music, family support, football, and snooker as formative parts of his development. The archive should present these as attributed life-history accounts, not as independently verified medical, school, or professional-sports records. The strongest wording is “he has said” or “public accounts report”; “professional footballer” and “professional snooker player” should not be used without a new named record.

## Anti-loop rule

Do not repeat exact-name searches for the same childhood phrases. Reopen a row only when one of these changes: a direct memoir or recording becomes accessible; a named school/club/tour appears; a host publishes a primary record; or a source gives a materially different date or formulation. Record the changed state in `research-log.md` and update the existing claim rather than creating a duplicate.
